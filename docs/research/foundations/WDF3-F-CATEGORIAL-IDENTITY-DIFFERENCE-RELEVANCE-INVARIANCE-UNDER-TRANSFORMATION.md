# 2. Numerical identity

Strict numerical identity answers:

```text
Are these one and the same referent rather than two?
```

Under the classical identity role, identity is:

```text
reflexive
symmetric
transitive
```

and satisfies the Indiscernibility of Identicals / Leibniz constraint:

```text
x = y
=>
anything genuinely true of x is genuinely true of y
```

with the usual care about time, modality, intensional contexts and representation.

### Important

These formal properties do **not** provide a universal substantive test for when two independently described candidates are identical.

They constrain a successful identity claim.

### F distinction

```text
IdentityLogic
!= IdentityCriterion
```

# 9. Claim invariance

Invariance is not primarily a relation of identity between two entities.

It is a statement of the form:

```text
Claim/property/structure P is unchanged under transformation tau
```

schematically:

```text
P(x) = P(tau(x))
```

or an appropriate structural analogue.

### Therefore

```text
Invariant(P,tau)
DOES NOT entail
x = tau(x)
```

The transformation may relate:

```text
two representations of one physical state
two distinct but symmetric physical states
two behaviorally equivalent programs
two phases with one preserved quantity
or two genuinely distinct entities sharing P
```

### Invariance/Identity Separation Law

# **IISL**

```text
Preservation of selected invariants under tau
is insufficient for numerical identity
unless an independent identity interpretation/criterion licenses it.
```

# 10. Gauge transformation: the strongest physical pressure

```text

-> same physical state
```

Mere mathematical intertransformability/isomorphism is insufficient.

Perfect mathematical copies can still represent different physical locations/states.

Thus:

```text
IntertransformableMathematically
!= GaugeEquivalentPhysically
```

and:

```text
GaugeCandidate(tau)
requires physical interpretation/evidence
```

### Gauge Relevance Rule

A transformation may support same-physical-state interpretation only when the changed mathematical features are independently justified as non-physical/surplus relative to the theory's physical content.

No purely formal quotient is enough.

# 23. Fission: the decisive identity falsifier

Suppose:

```text
A at t1
branches into distinct B and C at t2
```

and both B and C stand in equally strong continuity relation R to A.

If strict identity is transitive, then asserting:

```text
A = B
A = C
```

implies:

```text
B = C
```

which contradicts B and C being distinct.

Therefore:

# **Branching Identity Exclusion Law — BIEL**

```text
If B != C,
then one predecessor A cannot be numerically identical to both B and C.
```

### Consequence

```text
PerfectContinuityToBothBranches
!= IdentityToBothBranches
```

This is fatal to any theory that equates continuity/genealogy with identity without branching rules.

# 24. Fusion: the dual falsifier

Suppose distinct A and B fuse into C.


```text

```

contrary to the initial distinctness.

Thus:

# **Fusion Identity Exclusion Law — FIEL**

```text
Distinct predecessors cannot both be numerically identical to one successor.
```

### Consequence

Fusion/merge can preserve ancestry, contribution, continuation or institutional succession without preserving strict identity for every predecessor.

## Institutional-rule continuity

Powerful locally but does not apply to physics/biology generally.

Distinct isomorphic structures exist.

## Gauge equivalence

Requires domain-specific physical interpretation and is not universal.

# 67. Final compressed judgment

WDF3-F shows that `sameness` was still hiding several fundamentally different questions. Numerical identity is the strict one-and-the-same relation; qualitative sameness, equivalence, substitutability, invariance, continuity and persistence are not synonyms. Formal equivalence properties do not turn an equivalence relation into identity, and preserved invariants under a transformation do not determine whether the transformation is representational redundancy, gauge equivalence, physical symmetry, behavioral equivalence, implementation refinement or diachronic persistence.

The strongest cross-domain survivor is therefore not a universal identity criterion but a universal **identity logic constraint**: strict identity remains reflexive, symmetric, transitive and subject to indiscernibility of identicals. What varies by category and validity envelope are the constitutive conditions under which a diachronic or cross-description identity claim is warranted. This produces the new distinction `UniversalIdentityLogic != UniversalIdentityCriterion`.

Fission and fusion are decisive. A predecessor cannot be strictly identical to two later distinct branches, and two earlier distinct predecessors cannot both be strictly identical to one fused successor. Consequently genealogy, continuation, copying, branching and succession cannot be treated as identity merely because they preserve substantial structure. D's CategoryTrajectory must therefore be replaced by a typed CategoryContinuationGraph.

F also weakens E's literal invariant-preservation calculus. Cross-regime criteria are often transformed rather than literally preserved. The correct requirement is Target-Criterion Adjudicability: a bridge path and its licensed background must preserve, transport, transform or constitutionally supply enough Reality-grounded basis to decide the downstream criterion. TBW/BCW are therefore rebuilt as transformation- and identity-aware beta versions.

The remaining pressure is now temporal and lifecycle-specific. Having separated identity from continuity, WDF3 must determine what persistence actually requires across material replacement, interruption, restart, biological turnover, institutional succession, service migration, Agent forks, and category split/merge. That selects WDF3-G.
