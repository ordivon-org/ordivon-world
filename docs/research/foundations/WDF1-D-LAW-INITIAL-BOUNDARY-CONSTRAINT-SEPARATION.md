# WDF1-D — Law / Initial Condition / Boundary Condition / Constraint Separation

Status: complete for WDF1-D. No law metaphysics is selected. TARA remains provisional and survives with an important refinement: **nomological admissibility can remain theory-neutral only if it is treated as an interface/placeholder generated by a candidate law theory, not as a definition of lawhood.** WDF1-D rejects any attempt to identify lawhood with mathematical evolution form, generic history restriction, initial-data restriction or generic `constraint`. Exact next sub-round: **WDF1-E — Dynamic Reachability / Control / Intervention Architecture**.

## 0. Question

WDF1-C reconstructed a provisional modal meta-architecture:

```text
AlternativeDomain
+ typed Relation/Generator/Evaluator
+ typed Operator/Quantifier/Measure
+ Background/Dependence
(+ Anchor when relevant)
```

For objective physical modality it left one intentionally empty interface:

```text
R_nom / NomologicalAdmissibility
```

WDF1-D asks:

> What actually belongs on the nomological side of that interface, and what merely selects, bounds, realizes or queries a solution/history?

The primary falsification target is:

```text
Anything that restricts the set of possible histories is a law.
```

The secondary target is:

```text
Laws are exactly forward-time state-update equations.
```

Both are attacked across toy dynamics, variational/global formulations, general relativity, conservation/symmetry, thermodynamic boundary hypotheses, computation, material/resource constraints and institutions.

## 1. First separation — mathematical/solution role vs nomological status

WDF1-D finds that two orthogonal questions have repeatedly been conflated.

### Axis A — mathematical / solution role

A statement can function as:

```text
local evolution equation
constraint equation
initial condition
spatial boundary condition
terminal/final condition
variational principle
global/all-at-once consistency condition
symmetry/invariance statement
conservation/invariant relation
stochastic transition/kernel/measure specification
source/forcing/environment condition
target/query condition
```

### Axis B — nomological status

A statement can be treated by a candidate theory as:

```text
fundamental law
special-science/effective law
law consequence/theorem
contingent actuality condition
constitutive/material condition
environmental forcing/coupling
institutional rule
modeling assumption
query/target specification
```

### Major result

```text
MathematicalRole != NomologicalStatus
```

No entry on Axis A determines an entry on Axis B by syntax alone.

This becomes the central firewall of WDF1-D.

## 2. Case C1 — same law, different initial condition

Toy dynamics:

```text
x_{t+1} = x_t + 2
```

History family under the same transition rule includes:

```text
..., -2,0,2,4,...
..., -1,1,3,5,...
..., 0,2,4,6,...
```

depending on the chosen initial anchor.

Compare:

```text
World/model A: x_0 = 0
World/model B: x_0 = 1
```

The transition rule is unchanged while the realized/reachable history changes.

### Separation

```text
Law/DynamicsRole != InitialConditionRole
```

An initial condition selects/anchors a particular solution/history relative to the law/dynamics; it need not explain why the transition relation has that form.

## 3. Case C2 — same law and initial state, different spatial boundary condition

Take a wave/heat/PDE-style system on a spatial region. Hold fixed:

```text
differential equation
material parameters
initial field configuration
```

but change the boundary treatment:

```text
reflecting/fixed boundary
vs
absorbing/open boundary
```

The later solution differs although the bulk evolution equation remains the same.

### Separation

```text
BoundaryConditionRole != BulkEvolutionLawRole
```

A boundary condition describes how the modeled domain couples to its boundary/environment or which solution family is admitted; it is not automatically the same explanatory role as the bulk law.

## 4. Case C3 — target/terminal condition restricts histories but is not thereby law

Suppose a planner asks:

```text
Find trajectories satisfying x(T)=10.
```

The target condition:

```text
x(T)=10
```

removes all trajectories not ending at 10 from the **query solution set**.

It therefore restricts histories every bit as effectively as many equations do.

Yet the planner's desired target does not thereby become a law of nature.

### Separation

```text
RestrictsCandidateHistories
!=
Lawhood
```

This is the cleanest deletion test against the strongest `law = history restriction` formulation.

## 5. Case C4 — contingent environmental forcing also restricts realized histories

Take the same physical system under two external forcings:

```text
F_env(t)=0
vs
F_env(t)=sin(t)
```

The environment/forcing narrows or changes which actual trajectories occur.

But the force history can be contingent input rather than a fundamental law.

### Separation

```text
Causal/EnvironmentalInputThatRestrictsHistory
!=
LawOfNature
```

A history-selection role is therefore insufficient for lawhood even when the selector is physically real.

## 6. Case C5 — resource/capacity constraint restricts action histories but is Actor-relative

Suppose an actuator is physically governed by the same laws in two cases, but Actor A has:

```text
energy budget = 1 unit
```

and Actor B has:

```text
energy budget = 100 units.
```

Their feasible action-history sets differ.

The resource limit is real and action-relevant, but Actor-relative/current rather than a fundamental law.

### Separation

```text
ResourceConstraint
!=
NomologicalConstraint
```

This preserves WDF1-A's earlier `ConstraintRole != OneConstraintOntology` result.

## 7. Case C6 — institutional rule restricts admissible actions without being physical law

An institution can define:

```text
Authorized_A(action)=false
```

while the physical action remains possible.

Changing the rule through a valid institutional process can change authorization without changing physics.

### Separation

```text
InstitutionalRule
!=
LawOfNature
```

and:

```text
DeonticAdmissibility
!=
NomologicalAdmissibility
```

The two may compose in actionability but cannot share truthmaker by default.

## 8. `Restricts possible histories` fails as a sufficient condition for lawhood

C1–C6 give multiple non-law restrictors:

```text
initial condition
boundary condition
target condition
environmental forcing
resource constraint
institutional rule
```

All can reduce an admissible/selected trajectory set.

Therefore:

```text
RestrictsPossibleOrSelectedHistories(X)
↛
Law(X)
```

This is now a hard WDF1-D anti-law.

## 9. But history restriction remains an important representation of law consequences

Rejecting sufficiency does not mean laws cannot be represented by constraints on histories.

Chen & Goldstein's minimal primitivism explicitly develops a non-Humean governing view on which laws constrain physical possibilities without requiring fundamental forward-time production. Adlam likewise develops a modal/constraint framework motivated by nonlocal, atemporal, all-at-once and retrocausal law candidates.

Thus:

```text
CandidateLaw
→ may induce an admissible-history set/constraint
```

is a legitimate representation.

But the reverse reduction:

```text
Any history restriction
→ law
```

is rejected.

## 10. Case C7 — global variational principle breaks `law = local update rule`

The principle-of-least/stationary-action family evaluates entire paths/histories through an action functional and selects stationary histories subject to appropriate endpoint/boundary treatment.

This mathematical form is not naturally read as:

```text
state(t) mechanically produces state(t+dt)
```

although equivalent local Euler–Lagrange equations can often be derived under suitable assumptions.

Chen–Goldstein and Adlam both use variational/global candidates as pressure against the idea that governing law must be forward-directed state evolution.

### Separation

```text
Lawhood != ForwardTimeUpdateForm
```

and:

```text
GlobalConstraintForm can be nomological candidate
```

without implying all constraints are laws.

## 11. Case C8 — time-symmetric/all-at-once formulation breaks temporal-governance assumption

A fundamental theory can impose relations over an entire spacetime history or be time-symmetric while still being proposed as lawlike.

Therefore:

```text
FutureGeneratedFromPast
```

is not a necessary formal criterion for lawhood.

### Anti-law

```text
Governing != NecessarilyForwardTemporalProduction
```

This preserves WDF1-A's warning that `govern` is itself overloaded.

## 12. Case C9 — general relativity: constraint equations restrict initial data yet belong to the theory

In the initial-value formulation of Einstein's equations, arbitrary data on a spatial hypersurface are not allowed. Initial data must satisfy Einstein constraint equations; suitable data can then generate spacetime solutions of the full equations.

The key WDF1 lesson is structural:

```text
EquationThatConstrainsInitialData
```

can still be part of the physical theory's law structure rather than a contingent chosen initial condition.

### Separation

```text
ConstraintEquationRole
!=
ContingentInitialConditionRole
```

This directly blocks another simplistic mapping:

```text
constraint equation = background condition
```

## 13. C1 + C9 produce the two-axis necessity

C1:

```text
initial condition can restrict solution without being law.
```

C9:

```text
law/theory equation can restrict initial data without being initial condition.
```

Therefore the two dimensions cross:

```text
                    Nomological status
                 law/theory   contingent

Initial-data-like   C9-type     C1-type
mathematical role
```

This is why syntax alone cannot classify lawhood.

## 14. Case C10 — Past Hypothesis destroys `boundary condition = non-law`

The Past Hypothesis constrains the universe to a special low-entropy boundary/initial macrocondition. Eddy Chen explicitly argues that it is a candidate fundamental law of nature, despite its time-dependent/non-dynamical/boundary-condition character.

WDF1-D does **not** accept the Past Hypothesis as law by fiat.

Its role here is a falsifier:

```text
BoundaryConditionForm
↛
NonLawStatus
```

If a serious theory can consistently assign nomological status to a boundary-like condition, mathematical role does not decide metaphysical status.

## 15. Result — law vs condition requires a status theory, not a syntax rule

The following all fail:

```text
Law = differential equation
Law = evolution equation
Law = equation applying at every time
NonLaw = initial condition
NonLaw = boundary condition
Law = any constraint on histories
```

A future law theory must instead explain why some actual mathematical/physical structure has **nomological status** while other restrictions are contingent/model/query/institutional.

This is exactly what the rival law theories disagree about.

## 16. Case C11 — conservation law vs initial value

Suppose a system satisfies a conservation relation:

```text
E(t)=constant
```

The conservation relation restricts admissible evolution by requiring the same value throughout a trajectory.

But the **value** of the conserved quantity:

```text
E=7
```

can depend on the initial state.

Thus:

```text
ConservationForm: E(t)=E(0)
```

and:

```text
ConservedValue: E(0)=7
```

play different roles.

### Separation

```text
Invariant/ConservationRelation
!=
ParticularInvariantValue
```

The first may follow from law/symmetry structure; the second can be actuality/initial-data specific.

## 17. Case C12 — Noether pressure: symmetry and conservation are related but not identical labels

Noether's first theorem establishes a deep relation between continuous symmetries of variational problems and conservation laws/currents under the theorem's conditions.

For WDF1 the important lesson is not a blanket slogan `every symmetry = conservation law`, but:

```text
SymmetryStructure
can entail/organize
ConservationStructure
```

within a theory.

Therefore one should distinguish:

```text
symmetry of the action/law structure
conserved current/quantity derived under that symmetry
the particular value realized in one solution
```

### Anti-collapse

```text
Symmetry != ConservationLaw != ConservedValue
```

although the three can be systematically related.

## 18. Conservation relations can be nomic consequences rather than independent primitive laws

If a conservation theorem follows from deeper variational/symmetry structure, then writing both as unrelated fundamental primitives risks double-counting explanatory structure.

WDF1-D therefore introduces a status distinction:

```text
PrimitiveLawCandidate
DerivedNomicConsequence
```

without deciding which actual physical laws are primitive.

### Anti-law

```text
ImportantInvariant != AutomaticallyIndependentFundamentalLaw
```

## 19. Case C13 — model conservation constraint vs ontological law

A simulation may deliberately enforce:

```text
sum_i x_i = 1
```

through a numerical parameterization or constrained integrator.

The model constraint might represent a physical conservation law, a normalization convention or a computational design choice.

The same mathematical constraint therefore can have different truth roles.

### Result

```text
MathematicalConstraintForm != NomologicalTruthRole
```

Again, truth role must be explicit.

The relevant exclusion may arise from:

```text
+ current geometry
```

Example pattern:

### Separation

```text
ConstitutiveConstraint
```

This connects WDF1 to WDF0-E's realization/organization results.

## 21. Case C15 — protocol/computation: transition rule vs program input

Consider a deterministic program:

```text
output = f(input)
```

Hold code/semantics fixed and vary input:

```text
input A -> output A'
input B -> output B'
```

The program transition semantics constrain executions, while input selects one execution.

### Analogy pressure

```text
TransitionRuleRole != Input/InitialConditionRole
```

This does not prove laws are programs. It provides a clean non-physical matched case for the role distinction.

## 22. Case C16 — same program, different environment/configuration

A program can behave differently because of:

```text
filesystem contents
network response
environment variables
clock/random input
permissions
```

while the program code is unchanged.

These environmental conditions influence execution history without becoming program semantics by identity.

### Separation

```text
EnvironmentCondition != TransitionRule/ProgramSemantics
```

This is computational pressure against `everything that changes history = law`.

## 23. Case C17 — stochastic law vs token realization

Suppose a theory/model specifies a transition distribution:

```text
P(x_{t+1}|x_t)
```

The probabilistic law/kernel constrains/weights possible transitions.

The actual random/token outcome at one step selects one realized continuation.

Thus:

```text
ProbabilityLaw/Kernel
!=
TokenOutcome
```

and:

```text
DistributionalConstraint
!=
ActualRealization
```

WDF1-D still does not decide whether the probability represents objective chance, best-system probability, propensity or model uncertainty.

## 24. Case C18 — initial distribution vs stochastic transition law

A stochastic process may require both:

```text
initial distribution μ_0
transition kernel K
```

Changing `μ_0` with `K` fixed changes process-level predictions, while changing `K` changes transition structure.

### Separation

```text
InitialProbabilityDistributionRole
!=
StochasticTransitionLawRole
```

This mirrors deterministic initial-condition separation.

## 26. `Constraint` must split by source and truth role

WDF1-D now refines the overloaded word into at least these research roles:

### NC — nomological admissibility constraint

A restriction attributable to whatever the accepted law theory says is lawlike.

### IC — actuality/initial-data condition

A fact selecting actual initial data/history anchor.

### BC — boundary/coupling condition

A condition specifying interface/spatial/terminal coupling or endpoint treatment.

### INV — invariant/conservation relation

A relation preserved across admissible evolution, potentially derived from deeper law/symmetry.

### CONST — constitutive/material constraint

Restriction arising from realization/organization/material structure.

### ENV — contingent environment/forcing condition

External input/coupling specific to the actual situation.

### RES — Actor/resource/capacity constraint

Contextual feasibility restriction.

### DEON — institutional/deontic rule/status

Permission/obligation/authorization restriction or constitutive evaluation.

### EPI — epistemic/model constraint

Restriction in an Agent/model's represented alternative space.

### QRY — query/target condition

Restriction introduced by the question/optimization/problem itself.

### Major anti-law

```text
Constraint_X != Constraint_Y by default
```

The shared English word only expresses a meta-role: narrowing/structuring an alternative/query set.

## 27. Not all restrictions are subtractive filters

Some `constraints` generate/constitute structure rather than simply delete candidates.

Examples:

- a symmetry can organize equivalence classes/invariants;
- a constitutive rule can create an institutional action kind;
- a variational principle can define the extremal relation evaluated over histories;
- material organization can define available degrees of freedom.

Therefore:

```text
Constraint != SetSubtractionOnly
```

This protects TARA from reverting to universal nested filters.

## 28. Lawhood cannot be identified with invariance under time alone

A law candidate can be explicitly time-dependent or involve a special temporal boundary; the Past Hypothesis is an important philosophical example.

Conversely, many contingent structural facts can remain stable for long periods.

Thus:

```text
TimeInvariance
!=
Lawhood
```

and:

```text
TimeDependence
↛
NonLawhood
```

without further theory.

## 29. Lawhood cannot be identified with universality across systems alone

Fundamental-law candidates may be universal in scope, but effective/special-science laws are often regime-dependent. Conversely, a contingent mathematical identity can hold universally within a deliberately restricted model class.

Therefore:

```text
WideScope
!=
SufficientForLawhood
```

The relationship between fundamental and effective/special-science law remains open for later WDF1.

## 30. Lawhood cannot be identified with exceptionlessness alone

Special-science laws and idealized models often require domain/regime/ceteris-paribus qualification, while exact mathematical regularities can be accidental/model-constructed.

WDF1-D therefore refuses:

```text
ExceptionlessRegularity = Law
```

This leaves Humean Best-System and capacities/local-law rivals open rather than prejudging them.

## 31. Lawhood cannot be identified with explanation by fiat

Calling a pattern `law` can compress/explain phenomena in a theory, but explanatory usefulness alone is not sufficient to settle ontological lawhood.

A predictive model may explain operationally while being structurally wrong, as WDF0-D already established.

Thus:

```text
ExplanatoryUtility != NomologicalStatus
```

## 32. Lawhood cannot be identified with counterfactual stability yet

A tempting proposal is:

```text
Laws are what remain fixed across relevant counterfactuals.
```

But WDF1-A already exposed the circularity:

```text
laws determine which counterfactuals are relevant
counterfactual invariance is then used to characterize laws
```

WDF1-D therefore uses `law-held-fixed` only as a **counterfactual modeling convention** when a law theory is already supplied, not as a reduction of lawhood.

## 34. TARA refinement — NomologicalAdmissibility is an interface, not a definition

```text

```

meaning:

> according to candidate law account/specification `L`, history/configuration `h` belongs to the objectively law-admissible domain.

```text
Law(L) iff L restricts admissible histories
```

because C1–C18 falsify that reduction.

### Result

```text
TARA can remain law-metaphysics-neutral
```

This is a feature, not a failure. Modal architecture and law metaphysics are separable research layers.

## 36. Actuality specification and nomological admissibility should not collapse

WDF0 already retained Reality/Actuality upstream.

```text
LawSpace != ActualHistory
```

under theories with more than one admissible history.

## 38. Initial condition also remains theory-relative

In first-order deterministic evolution, complete state at one time may determine a unique trajectory.

In:

- higher-order systems;
- constrained systems;
- stochastic processes;
- gauge theories;
- global variational formulations;
- partial differential equations;

what counts as sufficient `initial data` differs.

Thus:

```text
InitialCondition != OneUniversalDataShape
```

It is a solution-role concept relative to the theory/formulation.

## 39. Conservation law can constrain reachability without being a separate transition rule

If a conserved quantity partitions state space into invariant sectors, then from actuality:

```text
x_actual ∈ sector S_E
```

states in another conserved-value sector may be dynamically unreachable.

This shows how:

```text
Invariant structure
→ reachability restriction
```

can arise without treating the invariant as a separate local transition rule.

This becomes important for WDF1-E.

## 40. Symmetry can organize alternative space

If a theory is invariant under transformation group G, then alternative configurations/histories can be grouped into orbits/equivalence classes under G, depending on whether the symmetry is physical or gauge/representational.

WDF1-D does not solve the physical-vs-gauge symmetry ontology.

It retains only:

```text
Symmetry can structure modal/solution space;
it is not merely another scalar constraint.
```

## 41. Gauge/representational warning

A transformation that changes mathematical description without changing physical state should not automatically generate a distinct physical alternative.

Therefore future physical TARA work must distinguish:

```text
mathematical configuration alternatives
from
physically distinct alternatives
```

This is an explicit secondary residual toward Space/Physicality foundations.

## 42. Determinism is not the same as lawhood

A law theory can be deterministic or stochastic.

Likewise, a deterministic model can use contingent/model-specific rules.

Therefore:

```text
Deterministic != Lawlike
Stochastic != NonLawlike
```

Determinism concerns how admissible histories are related to sufficient boundary/initial data under a theory, not whether the theory's rules count as laws.

## 43. Well-posedness is not lawhood either

A mathematical initial/boundary-value problem may be well-posed or ill-posed depending on equations, data and boundary treatment.

Well-posedness is crucial for prediction/computation but does not by itself decide which equations are laws of nature.

Thus:

```text
WellPosedModel != TrueLawTheory
```

This is another operational/ontological firewall.

## 44. Constraint source matters for intervention

Suppose target T is excluded.

Different source roles imply different possible remedies:

```text
NC nomological:
  target may require changing the theory/claim, not ordinary action

IC actuality:
  impossible to change past initial fact by present control; choose another target/current anchor

BC coupling:
  alter boundary/interface/environment if controllable

INV invariant:
  need interaction that exchanges conserved quantity with larger system or changes system boundary

CONST material:
  redesign structure/realizer

ENV forcing:
  alter/avoid environment input

RES resource:
  acquire capacity/complement

DEON institutional:
  obtain/change permission/role/rule

EPI epistemic:
  gather evidence/revise model

QRY target:
  change objective/question
```

This gives typed constraint theory immediate action-localization value.

## 45. Enlarging system boundary can change whether a conservation relation looks like a closed constraint

A subsystem may not conserve a quantity internally because it exchanges that quantity with its environment, while the larger closed system may satisfy a conservation relation.

Thus conservation claims inherit a boundary/system dependence signature.

### Anti-law

```text
ConservationClaim != BoundaryFreeAbsoluteStatement
```

unless the theory explicitly supplies one.

This is consistent with WDF0-D/E's boundary discipline.

## 46. Effective/material constraints can emerge from lower-level nomology + organization

An engineering object can have a strong effective constraint such as:

because of its geometry/material organization.

At another scale the restriction is realized by many lower-level physical interactions.

This creates:

```text
EffectiveConstraint_K
```

without requiring a new fundamental law.

### Result

```text
EffectiveConstraint can be objectively real/actionable
without being fundamental nomology.
```

This mirrors WDF0-E's macro-validity result.

## 47. Special-science law status remains open

Biology/economics/institutions use lawlike/general regularity claims whose domain, mechanisms and exception structure differ from idealized fundamental physics.

WDF1-D does not force them into:

```text
fundamental law
or
mere accident
```

The law-status hierarchy:


## 48. `Nature does not violate the law` is theory-language, not an empirical tautology

If observation conflicts with a supposed law, possibilities include:

```text
measurement/model error
wrong boundary/initial specification
unmodeled interaction
wrong effective regime
wrong law/theory
```

WDF1 therefore rejects a closed doctrine in which every discrepancy is automatically reclassified as `condition failure` to preserve a favored law.

### Research law

```text
LawCandidate must remain falsifiable/revisable at the model/theory level.
```

This is epistemic discipline, not a claim that fundamental Reality literally violates true laws.

## 49. Constraint hierarchy must not become ontology hierarchy

The taxonomy NC/IC/BC/INV/CONST/ENV/RES/DEON/EPI/QRY is a **truth-role/source taxonomy**.

It is not a claim that Reality consists of ten kinds of Constraint entities.

Many concrete facts can occupy multiple roles relative to different questions.

Example:

```text
a wall
```

can be:

- material constituent;
- geometric boundary;
- environmental obstacle;
- resource/capability constraint;
- observed evidence about route feasibility.

Same token, multiple roles.

## 50. Major result — lawhood is second-order status over a physical/mathematical structure

WDF1-D cannot yet define lawhood, but it can say what lawhood is **not**.

The strongest surviving characterization is:

> `Law` is not a mathematical shape or generic filtering operation. It is a nomological status assigned—under a candidate metaphysics/theory—to some physical/mathematical structure whose consequences constrain or organize physical possibilities.

This is intentionally incomplete.

Formally:

```text
Structure S
+ LawTheory Θ
→ NomologicalStatus_Θ(S)
→ consequences for admissible physical alternatives
```

Different rival theories disagree about `NomologicalStatus_Θ`:

- Best System: privileged systematizing role in the best system;
- necessitation: objective necessitation relation among universals;
- dispositional essentialism: grounded in property essences/powers;
- primitivism/constraint: fundamental nomic fact/constraint;
- plural/local accounts: potentially regime/model/capacity dependent.
