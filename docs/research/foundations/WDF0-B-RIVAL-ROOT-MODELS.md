# WDF0-B — Rival Root Models for World / Reality

Status: complete for WDF0-B. No World root ontology is frozen. WDF0-C cross-reality falsification is the next admitted sub-round. WDF1 remains UNKNOWN.

## 0. Research question

WDF0-A found that Ordivon already contains a latent meta-grammar around Reality, projection, boundary, evidence, authority, typed identity/currentness, resource/capability and action/effect, while remaining weak on physicality, space, general dynamics, causality, composition, information and system/environment.

WDF0-B asks:

> Which root grammar can reconstruct the greatest fraction of physical, biological, cognitive, social, institutional, computational and Agent-coupled reality with the fewest primitive commitments and the fewest destructive collapses?

The candidates are not treated as word choices. Each is required to explain the same reconstruction obligations.

## 1. Research discipline

A root model fails if it can describe a case only by silently importing the primitives it claims to eliminate.

Every rival must answer:

1. **Existence** — what sorts of things, occurrences or structures are admitted?
2. **Identity / persistence** — what makes something count as the same across time or transformation?
3. **Change** — what changes, and what does change mean?
4. **Time** — is time primitive, relational, a coordinate, or internal to process?
5. **Space / locality** — how are location, adjacency, containment and separation represented?
6. **Boundary / system** — how can inside/outside or part/whole be established?
7. **Causality** — what supports cause, mechanism, intervention, enabling condition and feedback?
8. **Constraint / possibility** — what distinguishes physically possible, reachable, feasible, permitted and realized?
9. **Information / representation** — what role, if any, does information have in Reality rather than in models of Reality?
10. **Resource / capability** — how can actor-relative actionability be reconstructed without turning it into a context-free substance?
11. **Composition / scale / emergence** — how do macro structures arise and retain explanatory or causal standing?
12. **Social / institutional reality** — how are firms, money, rights, law, roles and institutions real without either becoming free-floating abstractions or being reduced to irrelevant micro-details?
13. **Agent coupling** — how can observation, action and feedback occur without defining all Reality in Agent-relative terms?
14. **Model boundary** — does the candidate describe Reality, a useful model family, or both? The two must not be confused.

## 2. External rival pressure used in this round

### BFO / continuant–occurrent upper ontology

BFO 2020 is an ISO-oriented upper ontology whose top-level hierarchy distinguishes continuants from occurrents and explicitly includes material objects, qualities, roles/dispositions, processes, histories, spatial regions, temporal regions and fiat boundaries. It demonstrates that a rigorous scientific upper ontology need not reduce everything either to objects or to processes.

WDF0 use: serious object/category rival and evidence against single-category reduction.

### Process philosophy

Process philosophy treats dynamicity, becoming and occurrence as metaphysically central rather than merely secondary changes of static substances. Modern summaries explicitly extend process-oriented analysis across physical, organic, social and cognitive domains.

WDF0 use: serious challenge to state/object-first assumptions.

### Structural realism

Ontic structural realism raises the possibility that relational or structural organization can be at least as ontologically important as intrinsic individuals/properties. Strong eliminative variants face the classic `relations without relata` objection, while weaker structural views allow objects and structures to coexist.

WDF0 use: pressure on intrinsic-object ontology; not evidence that relations alone suffice.

### Dynamical-systems formalism

A dynamical system is commonly formalized as a state space, time and an evolution rule. Crucially, state-space coordinates are selected as what the modeler regards as sufficient to describe the system. Therefore state-space modeling is extremely powerful but does not by itself prove that Reality is ontologically made of states.

WDF0 use: strong modeling rival, weak direct metaphysical warrant.

### Markov blankets / multiscale system boundaries

Markov-blanket approaches provide statistical system/environment boundaries and can support nested boundaries across scales; the resulting boundary need not be co-extensive with ordinary biophysical boundaries.

WDF0 use: proof that a rigorous boundary can be relational/statistical and scale-sensitive rather than a unique spatial shell.

### Biological closure of constraints

The closure-of-constraints framework distinguishes open thermodynamic processes from relatively conserved constraints that channel them at a relevant timescale, with mutually dependent constraints contributing to biological organization and system boundaries.

WDF0 use: strong evidence that dynamics/process alone may omit organization, and that constraints can be causally relevant without being fundamental laws.

### Structural causal models

Structural causal models and intervention/counterfactual formalisms show that causal claims can be represented rigorously using variables, structural equations and intervention semantics. Their power also reveals a limit: they presuppose a choice of variables/model structure and therefore are not automatically a primitive ontology of Reality.

WDF0 use: causality as a cross-cutting explanatory grammar, not an automatically self-sufficient root.

### Scientific pluralism

Scientific pluralism rejects the assumption that one ontology, model, explanatory strategy or classification necessarily provides the single complete account of every scientific domain. Serious pluralist positions need not be relativist: multiple partial representations can remain answerable to mind-independent phenomena.

WDF0 use: `no single root noun` challenger; must still supply interoperability, conflict resolution and non-arbitrary admission rules.

## 3. M1 — Substance / Object / Property

### Root commitment

A strong form begins with something like:

```text
Reality = Objects / Individuals
        + Properties / Qualities
        + Relations
        + Changes of properties
```

A more mature version, exemplified by upper ontologies such as BFO, adds distinct categories for continuants and occurrents rather than pretending every process is merely a property update on an object.

### Reconstruction strengths

**Persistence.** Stable material bodies, instruments, documents, machines and organisms can be tracked naturally as continuants.

**Property bearing.** Mass, color, charge, location, legal role, software version or ownership status can be attached to a bearer under typed relations.

**Accountability / authority.** Legal and institutional systems often require persistent bearers to which claims, obligations, responsibility and rights attach.

**Engineering compatibility.** IDs, objects, records and resources map easily onto object-centric schemas.

### Reconstruction debts

#### Fire / vortex / storm / wave

The model must decide whether such phenomena are objects, processes, object aggregates, patterns or property configurations. If it imports `process` as an equally fundamental category, pure substance primacy is weakened.

#### Living organization

Organisms exchange much of their material while preserving organization and identity. Exact material sameness fails; object persistence therefore requires a typed criterion beyond substrate identity.

#### Social/institutional persistence

A corporation may retain legal identity while employees, shareholders, offices, servers and internal processes change. `same object = same matter` fails immediately.

#### Dynamic phenomena

Treating change solely as successive property assignments risks describing trajectories without explaining becoming, causal organization or path dependence.

### Hidden imports required by the strong object model

To survive WDF0 cases, M1 normally imports:

- process/event;
- relation;
- time;
- typed persistence;
- boundary;
- modal/causal structure.

At that point the root claim `Objects are enough` no longer survives.

### WDF0-B disposition

```text
Pure substance/object monism: REJECT as World root.
Typed continuant + occurrent category grammar: SURVIVES as a serious plural ontology rival.
```

Key anti-law:

```text
PersistentBearer != UniversalFormOfExistence
```

## 4. M2 — Event / Process / Becoming

### Root commitment

```text
Reality = Processes / Occurrences / Events
Objects  = relatively stable organizations or patterns within process
```

Time/change are not secondary metadata; they are constitutive of existence.

### Reconstruction strengths

**Fire, weather, metabolism, evolution.** Naturally described as ongoing processes rather than awkward objects.

**Human development / learning.** Fits HF6–HF7 history-dependent change better than a snapshot-only ontology.

**Markets / institutions.** Trading, production, governance, law enforcement and organizational reproduction can be seen as recurring processes maintaining higher-level patterns.

**Computing.** Execution, communication and distributed service behavior are inherently temporal occurrences.

**History.** Persistence can be modeled as temporal coherence of an organized process rather than static substance.

### Reconstruction debts

#### Stable bearer problem

Rights, obligations, mass, location, ownership and identity are often predicated of something treated as a continuing bearer. A pure process ontology must reconstruct why some processes count as the same continuing entity and support durable reference.

#### Simultaneous structural facts

At one moment, network topology, legal hierarchy, spatial adjacency or account ownership may matter as structural constraints independent of narrating their full history.

#### Institutional identity

A company, university or state is more than an arbitrary temporal sequence. The process model needs criteria for organizational continuity, boundary and role inheritance.

#### Spatial persistence

Processes still occur somewhere or across spatial relations; space/locality do not disappear.

### Hidden imports required by strong process monism

- pattern / structure;
- persistence criterion;
- relation;
- constraint;
- boundary;
- spatial organization.

### WDF0-B disposition

```text
Process/event as indispensable ontic family: SURVIVES.
Process monism claiming all other categories are dispensable: NOT YET JUSTIFIED.
```

Key result:

```text
ObjectStability may be process-supported,
but Process alone does not yet reconstruct all bearer/structure claims.
```

## 5. M3 — Relation / Structure First

### Root commitment

```text
Reality = relational / structural organization
Identity and properties are substantially determined by position in structures
```

Strong variants reduce or eliminate intrinsic individuals; weaker variants give relations/structure ontological priority without eliminating relata.

### Reconstruction strengths

**Finance.** Rights, claims, obligations, positions, funding and market structure are deeply relational.

**Capability.** Human/World capability already has the form `Actor × Outcome × Conditions × Access`.

**Networks.** Connectivity, topology, dependency and communication are structurally natural.

**Institutional reality.** Role, authority, office, legal standing and social status often depend on relational position rather than intrinsic material composition.

**Physics pressure.** Modern structural-realism debates demonstrate that relation/structure cannot be dismissed as merely secondary descriptive convenience.

### Reconstruction debts

#### Relations-without-relata

Strong eliminativism faces the basic question: what is related? A relation graph with no account of node/bearer individuation risks becoming formally empty.

#### Intrinsic/local state

Some distinctions appear to require local qualities or dispositions, not only position within a relation network.

#### Change

Structure can change, but a static structural ontology needs temporal/dynamical machinery to explain transformation and history.

#### Material/energetic substrate

Network structure alone does not explain why a bridge supports weight, a battery stores chemical energy or a server dissipates heat.

### WDF0-B disposition

```text
Relation/structure as indispensable family: SURVIVES strongly.
Eliminative relation-only ontology: FAILS current reconstruction burden.
```

Key anti-law:

```text
RelationalImportance != RelataElimination
```

## 6. M4 — State Space / Dynamics

### Root commitment

A generic dynamical form is:

```text
StateSpace S
Time T
Evolution rule F

s(t+Δt) = F(s(t), Δt, parameters, inputs...)
```

or continuous analogues.

### Reconstruction strengths

**Prediction/control.** Excellent for physical dynamics, control systems, game worlds, population models, finance and many computational systems.

**Discrete and continuous compatibility.** Can describe state machines, flows, stochastic processes and hybrid systems.

**Trajectory.** Gives precise temporal evolution and supports attractors, stability, bifurcation and reachability.

**Agent planning.** POMDP/MDP/control-style models naturally use state/action/observation transitions.

### Critical finding

The mathematical definition of state space explicitly depends on a coordinate choice regarded as sufficient for the modeling objective. Therefore:

```text
StateSpace = model representation of a target system
```

is secure, while:

```text
Reality itself = one privileged StateSpace
```

is not established.

### Reconstruction debts

#### State-variable selection

Which variables are state? Temperature and pressure? Molecular microstate? Institutional status? Beliefs? The answer changes with scale/model purpose.

#### Open-world novelty

New entities, variables, institutions, goals or technologies can alter the relevant state space itself. Fixed-dimensional closure is not universally safe.

#### Identity

A state vector describes configuration but does not by itself explain persistence criteria of the systems/components represented.

#### Meaning / institution

A bit pattern or state variable does not acquire legal or semantic meaning merely by being part of the state.

#### Boundary

A dynamical system is normally specified after a system and variable boundary has already been chosen.

### WDF0-B disposition

```text
State-space/dynamics: RETAIN as exceptionally powerful modeling grammar.
State as universal ontological primitive: REJECT for now.
```

Major anti-law:

```text
CompleteStateRelativeToModel != CompleteReality
```

## 7. M5 — System / Environment / Boundary

### Root commitment

```text
System S
Environment E_S
Boundary B_S
Internal organization
External coupling
```

Reality may be decomposed into interacting systems, potentially nested or overlapping across scales.

### Reconstruction strengths

**Life.** Organism/environment distinctions, self-maintenance and regulation require a system boundary of some kind.

**Agents.** Observation and action become boundary-crossing relations between internal and external states/processes.

**Computing.** Process/container/service/machine/network boundaries and interfaces fit naturally.

**Organizations.** Firms, governments and institutions have internal role structures plus external exchanges and dependencies.

**Nested scale.** Markov blankets and other formalisms show that statistically meaningful boundaries can exist at multiple scales.

### Reconstruction debts

#### Boundary criterion

Why is one partition the system? Spatial enclosure, causal insulation, statistical conditional independence, organizational closure, legal jurisdiction and task relevance yield different boundaries.

#### Overlap

A person can simultaneously be inside a biological organism boundary, family system, firm, legal jurisdiction and digital task system. One total partition is insufficient.

#### Non-system background

If everything is called a system, the concept becomes vacuous. Reality must not be forced to have one privileged decomposition into systems.

#### Boundary existence vs boundary utility

Some boundaries are strongly substrate-supported; others are analytical, institutional or goal-relative. The grammar must distinguish them.

### WDF0-B disposition

```text
System/environment: SURVIVES as a major relational/partition grammar.
World = one unique system/environment partition: REJECT.
```

Provisional relation:

```text
Environment_S = Reality outside/around a typed SystemBoundary_S
                insofar as it is relevantly coupled to S
```

This is a candidate, not frozen law.

## 8. M6 — Constraint / Possibility / Modal Structure

### Root commitment

Instead of starting from what things are, start from what configurations/trajectories are possible and what restricts them:

```text
PossibilitySpace P
Constraints C
Conditions K
Realized trajectory τ ⊂ P
```

Constraints may be:

- physical law / conservation;
- geometry/topology;
- boundary condition;
- material structure;
- resource scarcity;
- organizational constraint;
- legal/institutional rule;
- authority/permission;
- information/access limitation.

### Reconstruction strengths

**Capability / option.** Directly supports the Ordivon chain from Reality conditions to reachable/feasible/authorized actions.

**Biological organization.** Closure-of-constraints work shows a concrete domain where relatively conserved constraints channel open thermodynamic processes and help define organismal organization.

**Institutions.** Rights, law, policy and authority often act by altering admissible action/effect spaces rather than by behaving like ordinary material objects.

**Physical systems.** Laws, symmetries, boundary conditions and conservation constrain trajectories.

**Engineering.** Resource limits, security policy and protocol validity are naturally modal/constraint-like.

### Reconstruction debts

#### Actuality problem

A possibility space plus constraints does not say which actual configuration exists or which event occurs without a realization/dynamics component.

#### Bearer/source problem

What instantiates or enforces a constraint? Physical law, material boundary, legal rule and access policy have radically different truth/causal roles.

#### Possibility-relative-to-model problem

What counts as possible depends on which variables, scales and rules are included. Model possibility must not be silently promoted to metaphysical possibility.

#### Persistence problem

Constraints can help explain stability but do not alone define identity of enduring systems.

### WDF0-B disposition

```text
Constraint/possibility grammar: SURVIVES strongly as a cross-cutting modal family.
Constraint-only ontology: INSUFFICIENT without actuality/dynamics/bearers.
```

Important candidate chain:

```text
Possible
→ Reachable
→ Feasible
→ Accessible
→ Authorized
→ Selected
→ Realized
```

The distinctions are not yet frozen and may be actor/context relative.

## 9. M7 — Interaction / Causality First

### Root commitment

```text
Reality = causally interacting variables / entities / events / mechanisms
```

Causal structure determines how intervention or change propagates.

### Reconstruction strengths

**Action/effect.** Directly connects Ordivon's operational grammar to world change.

**Mechanism.** Supports explanation beyond mere correlation.

**Counterfactuals/interventions.** Structural causal models formalize questions such as what would happen under a changed input/policy.

**Cross-scale relevance.** Causal modeling can evaluate whether macro variables carry stable intervention-relevant structure.

### Reconstruction debts

#### Causal variable ontology

Structural causal models need variables and equations chosen by a modeler. They do not decide by themselves what the world's canonical variables are.

#### Non-causal relations

Spatial, mathematical, constitutive, semantic, logical and legal relations are not all reducible to efficient-causal arrows.

#### Symmetry / equilibrium / static structure

A current geometry or institutional classification can be real/relevant without being exhausted by a narrative of causal production.

#### Cause meaning diversity

Mechanism, intervention, enabling condition, prevention, omission, constraint and constitutive explanation may not reduce to one relation.

### WDF0-B disposition

```text
Causality/interaction: SURVIVES as indispensable explanatory grammar.
Causality as sole root ontology: REJECT for now.
```

Key anti-law:

```text
CausalGraph != WorldOntology
```

## 10. M8 — Plural / No Single Root Noun

### Root commitment

This challenger rejects the assumption that one noun must dominate:

```text
Reality may require several irreducible ontic families
and several typed projections at different scales/purposes.
```

Potential families might include persistent bearers, processes, relations, constraints, spatial/temporal structures and higher-level organizations, but WDF0-B does not freeze such a list.

### Reconstruction strengths

**Cross-domain compatibility.** Allows physics, biology, cognition, institutions and computation to retain different useful ontologies without forcing premature reduction.

**Matches Ordivon evidence.** Human HF0 and Finance v1 already show that multiple projections can be jointly necessary while familiar high-level nouns remain query-relative.

**Avoids fake unification.** Does not rename every phenomenon into a universal `Entity`, `State`, `Event` or `Interaction` object.

**Scale sensitivity.** Can retain microphysical and macro-organizational descriptions when both are empirically/projectively indispensable.

### Reconstruction debts

#### Trivial-pluralism risk

If every domain gets arbitrary primitives, no compression or discovery occurs.

#### Conflict resolution

Two models may make incompatible claims. A serious plural grammar needs criteria for scope, evidence, cross-level consistency and contradiction.

#### Interoperability

Ordivon still needs common bridges for cross-domain action. Pluralism cannot become `nothing can be compared`.

#### Ontic vs representational pluralism

Multiple useful models do not prove that Reality itself contains multiple irreducible ontological categories. WDF0 must keep this distinction open.

### WDF0-B disposition

```text
No-single-root/plural architecture: SURVIVES as strongest meta-level challenger.
Unconstrained ontology pluralism: REJECT.
```

Key requirement:

```text
Plurality must be typed, scoped, evidence-constrained and composition-aware.
```

## 12. The most important WDF0-B discovery — the rivals are not all at the same logical level

The original WDF0 plan treated all candidates as if they were competing answers to one question. WDF0-B falsifies that framing.

They separate into at least four truth-role families:

### 12.1 Ontic-category candidates

```text
persistent bearer / continuant
process / occurrence
elation / structure
```

These answer primarily: **what kinds of reality are there?**

### 12.2 Modeling / dynamical grammar

```text
state space
trajectory
evolution rule
```

These answer primarily: **how can a selected system be represented through change?**

They can be ontologically suggestive, but their coordinates remain model-bound.

### 12.3 Partition / organization grammar

```text
system
environment
boundary
part / whole
organization
scale
```

These answer primarily: **how is some portion of Reality individuated or organized?**

### 12.4 Modal / causal grammar

```text
constraint
possibility
cause
mechanism
intervention
affordance / capability
```

These answer primarily: **what can happen, why does it happen, and what changes the space of possible effects?**

### 12.5 Meta-representational discipline

```text
projection
model
scope
plurality
evidence
```

These answer primarily: **how do our claims about Reality remain bounded and falsifiable?**

This yields a major correction:

> World Foundations may fail if it searches for one primitive noun because several candidate concepts occupy different explanatory dimensions rather than the same taxonomic slot.

## 13. New candidate architecture exposed by WDF0-B

WDF0-B does **not** freeze a final grammar, but the rival comparison suggests that a successful World foundation may need something closer to a multi-axis architecture:

```text
Reality
│
├─ Ontic standing
│   ├─ persistent bearer / pattern
│   ├─ occurrence / process
│   └─ relation / structure
│
├─ Organization / partition
│   ├─ part-whole
│   ├─ boundary
│   ├─ system-environment
│   └─ scale
│
├─ Dynamics
│   ├─ configuration / state projection
│   ├─ change
│   ├─ trajectory
│   └─ history
│
├─ Modal / causal structure
│   ├─ constraints
│   ├─ possibilities
│   ├─ causes / mechanisms
│   └─ interventions / effects
│
└─ Epistemic projection
    ├─ observation
    ├─ evidence
    ├─ representation
    ├─ belief / claim
    └─ model
```

This is a **candidate decomposition of question types**, not a claim that Reality literally consists of five modules.

## 15. Which models are actually falsified in B?

WDF0-B can reject several stronger claims before WDF0-C:

### Rejected 1 — Object monism

```text
Everything important is reducible to persistent objects and their properties.
```

Fails processual, organizational and institutional cases without importing process/relation/constraint.

### Rejected 2 — Process monism

```text
Persistent bearer, structure, boundary and institution are fully dispensable once processes are described.
```

Not demonstrated; durable reference and organization require extra criteria.

### Rejected 3 — Relation eliminativism

```text
Relations/structure are sufficient without any account of relata/bearers/occurrences.
```

Fails current reconstruction burden.

### Rejected 4 — State ontologization

```text
A useful state-space description proves that Reality is a state vector plus transition rule.
```

Conflates representation with ontology.

### Rejected 5 — Unique system partition

```text
Reality has one privileged System / Environment split for every target.
```

Contradicted by typed, nested and overlapping boundaries.

### Rejected 6 — Constraint monism

```text
Constraints and possibility space alone determine actuality, identity and material organization.
```

Requires realization/dynamics/bearers.

### Rejected 7 — Causal-graph ontology

```text
A causal graph is the ontology of Reality.
```

Variables and structure are model-selected; noncausal relations remain.

### Rejected 8 — Unconstrained pluralism

```text
Different domains may use arbitrary incompatible ontologies with no shared discipline.
```

Would destroy cross-domain reasoning and falsifiability.

## 16. What survives B

WDF0-B retains the following **families**, not frozen primitives:

1. **Persistence / bearer / pattern** — some realities support durable reference across change.
2. **Occurrence / process / change** — some realities are intrinsically temporal and cannot be treated as static property bundles.
3. **Relation / structure** — relational position and organization can be constitutive or causally/explanatorily indispensable.
4. **Boundary / system / environment** — typed partitions are required for many explanations, but are not uniquely global.
5. **State / trajectory / dynamics** — essential representational grammar, with explicit model-bound coordinates.
6. **Constraint / possibility** — essential for law, organization, feasibility, institution and capability.
7. **Causality / mechanism / interaction** — essential for action/effect and explanation, but not self-sufficient ontology.
8. **Projection plurality / evidence discipline** — required to prevent one successful formalism from becoming fake universal ontology.

## 19. Exact WDF0-B conclusion

```text

```

The second result is more surprising:

```text
Object, Process, Relation, State, System, Constraint and Causality
are not clean peers.
They answer different kinds of foundational questions.
```

Therefore the likely search target changes from:

```text
Find the one thing Reality is made of.
```

toward:

```text
Find the smallest orthogonal grammar that preserves
existence/persistence, occurrence/change, relation/structure,
organization/boundary, modal/causal possibility,
and epistemic projection without destructive collapse.
```

This is not yet the WDF0 reconstruction. WDF0-C must now attack whether even these families are genuinely irreducible across radically different realities.

## 20. WDF0-B closeout

WDF0-B is complete under the following state:

```text
Pure monisms are provisionally rejected.
A typed plural grammar is favored as a research architecture,
not yet as a metaphysical conclusion.

State-space is demoted from hidden ontology to explicit modeling grammar.
System/environment is retained as typed partition grammar.
Constraint/possibility and causality are retained as modal/explanatory grammars.
Object/process/relation remain competing ontic families.

WDF0-C must determine which of these survive cross-reality deletion tests.
WDF1 remains UNKNOWN.
```
