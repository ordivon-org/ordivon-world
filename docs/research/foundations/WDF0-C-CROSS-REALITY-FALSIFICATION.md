# WDF0-C — Cross-Reality Falsification and Deletion Tests

Status: complete for WDF0-C. No positive World ontology is frozen. WDF0-D observer/model audit is the next admitted sub-round. WDF1 remains UNKNOWN.

## 0. Question

WDF0-B rejected pure object, process, relation, state, system, constraint and causal monisms and found that these candidates occupy different explanatory truth roles. WDF0-C now asks a harder question:

> Which explanatory families remain genuinely necessary when the same grammar is forced through physical, biological, cognitive, institutional, computational and Agent-coupled reality?

The aim is not to prove metaphysical primitives. The aim is to identify **deletion failures**: if removing a family destroys an explanation or forces the explanation to secretly recreate an equivalent family, that role survives to later WDF0 rounds.

## 1. Candidate family set entering C

WDF0-B carries the following families:

```text
P — Persistence / bearer / pattern
O — Occurrence / process / change
R — Relation / structure
B — Boundary / system / organization / scale
D — State / trajectory / dynamics / history
M — Constraint / possibility / modality
C — Causality / mechanism / interaction
E — Epistemic projection / observation / evidence / model
```

C also permits **new residuals** if real cases require structure not contained in this set.

## 2. Deletion-test discipline

For a target phenomenon X and candidate family F:

```text
Explain(X | all admitted families)
then remove F
then ask:

1. Can the phenomenon still be individuated?
2. Can its persistence/change be distinguished?
3. Can its causal/constitutive explanation still be stated?
4. Can counterfactual or intervention questions still be answered?
5. Can the same observations be distinguished from a different reality?
6. Did the replacement explanation secretly rename F?
```

A family survives when deletion repeatedly causes information loss or hidden reintroduction. Survival does **not** mean every Reality statement must instantiate that family.

## 3. C1 — Physical falsifiers

### 3.1 Ballistic body

A rigid projectile is the easiest object-friendly case.

Useful description:

```text
body identity
+ position / momentum projection
+ trajectory through spacetime
+ forces / interactions
+ physical constraints / laws
```

#### Delete P — persistence

Without a continuing referent, the positions at successive times cannot be asserted to belong to the same projectile. One can replace a bearer with a worldline, but the worldline itself is a persistence/continuity structure.

Result: **P survives**, but as typed continuity rather than substance metaphysics.

#### Delete O/D — process/dynamics

A list of disconnected positions cannot distinguish actual motion from an arbitrary collection of locations. Dynamics or trajectory is required.

Result: **O/D survive**.

#### Delete spatial locality

None of P/O/R/B/D/M/C/E as currently named explicitly captures metric/geometric relations such as location, direction and distance. Encoding them merely as generic `Relation` loses physically important invariance and locality structure.

Result: **new residual S — spatial/locality/geometry** is exposed.

### 3.2 Flame

A flame is a stronger anti-object falsifier.

The material constituents continuously enter and leave the reaction region. The phenomenon persists through organized combustion, transport, chemical reaction and energy flow rather than exact matter conservation within a fixed object shell.

#### Delete P

A particular flame can still be tracked across material turnover, so `persistent material bearer` is unnecessary. But some continuity criterion—spatiotemporal continuity of organized reaction/flow—is required if we want to say this is the same ongoing flame rather than unrelated combustion events.

Result:

```text
MaterialSameness is deleted.
TypedPattern/ProcessContinuity survives.
```

#### Delete O

The flame collapses into a static arrangement. Combustion is intrinsically processual.

Result: **O is irreducible for this case**.

#### Delete R/B

The spatial relation among reaction zone, fuel, oxidizer, heat transport and flow matters. A bag of reaction events is not yet a flame. Organized structure and a question-relative boundary are needed.

Result: **R/B survive**.

#### Delete M

Fuel availability, stoichiometry, ignition thresholds, geometry and thermodynamic conditions constrain whether flame propagation is possible.

Result: **M survives**.

#### Delete physical quantities/flows

Generic relation/process language is too thin if mass, energy, chemical species and fluxes are erased. A physically realized flame differs from an isomorphic abstract event graph because its quantities and conservation/transfer relations matter.

Result: **new residual Q — physical quantity/substrate/flow** is exposed.

### 3.3 Vortex / turbulent coherent structure

A vortex is not well tracked by constituent matter: fluid parcels enter and leave while a coherent rotating structure can persist and propagate.

Deletion results:

- exact material bearer: dispensable;
- pattern persistence: required;
- process/dynamics: required;
- spatial topology/geometry: required;
- relation/structure: required;
- boundary: often graded or diagnostic rather than hard shell;
- scale: essential because `vortex` appears only under a coarse description of a flow field.

Key anti-law:

```text
PersistentPhenomenon != PersistentConstituentMatter
```

### 3.4 Wave

A wave similarly demonstrates propagation of pattern without transport of one persistent material object across the entire path.

Deletion failures:

- O/D: without temporal evolution there is no propagation;
- S: wavelength, phase, direction and propagation require spatial/temporal geometry;
- R: phase relationships and coupling matter;
- P: a wave packet can be tracked by pattern continuity, not by constituent identity.

This case reinforces a candidate deeper account of persistence:

```text
Persistence_K may be invariance/continuity of selected structure
under admissible transformation, rather than material sameness.
```

### 3.5 Phase transition

Phase transitions attack both naive object and naive state ontologies.

Microscopic constituents can remain the same while a macroscopic order changes. Order parameters and phase labels are scale/coarse-graining dependent descriptions, yet they can support robust prediction and causal intervention at the macro level. Modern work on symmetry-breaking and nonequilibrium phase transitions continues to show that collective order can change qualitatively without the phenomenon reducing to a simple object identity change.

Deletion results:

- scale/coarse-graining: cannot be deleted without losing the phase distinction;
- D: phase/state projection is useful but depends on selected macro variables;
- R: collective organization/correlation matters;
- M: symmetry/law/boundary conditions constrain available phases;
- O: transition is a process even when each phase is represented statically.

Key result:

```text
MacroState may be explanatory and physically grounded
without being the unique complete state of Reality.
```

### 3.6 Open thermodynamic system

An open system exchanges matter and/or energy across a selected boundary. This attacks the assumption that a system is a self-contained object.

Deletion failures:

- B: no open-system statement exists without a boundary relative to which flux is measured;
- Q: mass/energy/particle/entropy flows cannot be reduced to unlabeled generic edges;
- O/D: flux and dissipation are temporal;
- M: conservation laws and constitutive/boundary conditions constrain trajectories;
- R: system–environment coupling is constitutive of openness.

### 3.7 C1 physical result

Physical cases preserve B's plural grammar but reveal that B was incomplete.

New explicit residuals:

```text
S — Spatial / locality / geometry / topology
Q — Physical substrate / quantity / flow / exchange
```

Neither is yet declared metaphysically primitive. They are **description roles that cannot be erased** when World claims concern physical realization.

Physical anti-laws:

1. `PersistentPhenomenon != PersistentMatter`.
2. `Process != SequenceOfStaticSnapshots`.
3. `GenericRelation != SufficientPhysicalGeometry`.
4. `AbstractCausalGraph != PhysicalRealization`.
5. `MacroState != MerePresentation` by default.
6. `MacroState != CompleteMicroReality`.
7. `SystemBoundary != MaterialShell`.
8. `PhysicalRealization requires typed quantities/flows when those quantities are causally relevant`.

## 4. C2 — Biological falsifiers

### 4.1 Cell

A living cell provides a dense collision of all B families.

A membrane provides a physically realized boundary, but cellular identity is not just membrane geometry. Metabolism, repair, transport and regulation continuously replace material. Biological organization theories such as closure-of-constraints distinguish non-equilibrium processes from relatively conserved constraints that channel those processes at relevant timescales, and use their mutual maintenance to characterize organization and sometimes system boundaries.

Deletion results:

- exact material sameness: fails;
- process: cannot be deleted because metabolism/self-maintenance cease to exist;
- organization/constraint: cannot be deleted without reducing a living cell to unconstrained chemistry;
- boundary: required but maintained dynamically rather than merely given;
- timescale: essential because a structure can count as constraint only relative to slower conservation than the processes it constrains;
- causality: regulatory/mechanistic dependencies matter.

Major result:

```text
Boundary can be produced and maintained by organization.
Boundary need not be a prior primitive cut.
```

### 4.2 Organism under material turnover

An organism replaces matter while maintaining biological continuity. Human HF1 already rejects exact material identity as a universal persistence relation.

Deletion result:

```text
material identity can be removed;
organizational, lineage, physiological and/or organismic continuity cannot all be removed.
```

This strengthens the candidate:

```text
Identity_K(x_t1,x_t2)
≈ preservation of K-relevant invariants/continuities
through an admissible transformation history.
```

The exact invariants depend on the persistence question; no one K is universal.

### 4.3 Development

Development attacks fixed-state-space closure.

A developing organism does not merely move through different values of a permanently fixed set of meaningful variables. New tissues, organs, regulatory relations and capabilities emerge; the variables that are explanatorily useful can themselves change.

Deletion failures:

- history: current structure cannot be inferred only from a coarse current snapshot when developmental path matters;
- O/D: development is trajectory, not state label;
- B/R: organization changes qualitatively;
- M: developmental constraints and possibilities change over time.

Key anti-law:

```text
FixedStateSpace != OpenEndedDevelopment
```

A state-space model may still be constructed, but its adequacy is a modeling achievement, not a foundational assumption.

### 4.4 Ecosystem

Ecosystems attack hard-boundary and context-free-state assumptions.

Empirical and theoretical ecology documents alternative stable states, hysteresis, resilience and state changes dependent on feedback and history. Recent forest work, for example, reports positive feedbacks sufficient in simulations to generate alternative forest states and history dependence during transitions.

Deletion results:

- history: cannot be removed under hysteresis;
- interaction/network: cannot be removed because species/environment feedbacks generate system behavior;
- scale: ecosystem-level properties are not identical to a single organism's state;
- boundary: ecosystem boundary is often study/flow/function-relative rather than a membrane-like shell;
- state: useful at ecosystem scale, but a selected macro projection;
- M/C: feedback and constraints shape stability basins and transitions.

Key result:

```text
SameExternalConditions can support different current macro realities
when history and internal organization differ.
```

### 4.5 Evolutionary lineage / species

Species falsify static class ontology.

The general lineage approach treats species as separately evolving metapopulation lineages, while properties such as reproductive isolation, diagnosability or monophyly function as evidence for lineage separation rather than interchangeable definitions. Other work continues to dispute whether lineage alone captures all biodiversity-relevant structure.

The important World result is not to settle the species problem. It is:

```text
CategoryConcept != DelimitationEvidence
```

and:

```text
HistoricalLineage can be constitutive of what a biological unit is.
```

Deletion failures:

- genealogy/history: severe;
- population relation/structure: severe;
- evidence/projection separation: severe;
- boundary: often fuzzy and evidence-dependent, not an instantaneous spatial shell.

### 4.6 C2 biological result

C2 elevates **History / genealogy / path dependence** from optional metadata to a recurrent constitutive coordinate.

It also sharpens B:

```text
Organization != StaticStructure
Organization may be recursively maintained through processes and constraints.
```

Biological anti-laws:

1. `OrganismIdentity != MaterialSameness`.
2. `Boundary != AlwaysPriorToOrganization`.
3. `CurrentSnapshot != DevelopmentalExplanation`.
4. `CurrentState != FullHistory`.
5. `SpeciesLabel != SpeciesReality`.
6. `DelimitationEvidence != OntologicalDefinition`.
7. `Function != GoalOfExternalDesigner` by default.
8. `Timescale is constitutive to some constraint/organization claims`.

## 5. C3 — Cognitive / Human falsifiers

### 5.1 Transient experience

A conscious experience is an occurrence with content. Human Foundations already separates experience from report, responsiveness, memory and third-person evidence.

Deletion results:

- O: deleting occurrence destroys the phenomenon;
- E: deleting evidence-position distinctions collapses experience into report/measurement;
- persistent bearer: the Human/subject may persist, but the particular experience need not;
- representation/content: describing only causal neural activity omits the claim that an experience has content for a subject.

C3 therefore exposes a residual not cleanly handled by B:

```text
I — Information / representation / content / meaning
```

This family must later be decomposed; it is currently intentionally overloaded.

### 5.2 Memory-supported identity

Memory provides a powerful transformation falsifier. Experimental work on engrams shows that memory representations can remain behaviorally meaningful while the neural composition of the engram changes during consolidation; neurons can drop into and out of the engram while selectivity emerges.

Deletion implications:

- exact substrate identity: dispensable;
- historical/causal organization: required;
- representational identity: cannot be equated with fixed cell membership;
- current retrieval failure: does not imply erased history, matching Human HF7.

This supports:

```text
RepresentationPersistence != SubstrateComponentPersistence
```

and further supports typed invariance across transformation.

### 5.3 Skill learning

A skill can persist while not currently being performed and can be masked by fatigue, context or unavailable tools.

Deletion results:

- current behavior: insufficient;
- historical plasticity/learning: required for explaining changed disposition;
- M: capability/disposition is modal—it concerns classes of possible outcomes under conditions;
- R/B: realized skill depends on actor–environment–tool coupling.

Thus:

```text
Capability != CurrentAction
Capability != MerePastSuccess
```

Capability is a typed disposition/relation in a possibility space.

### 5.4 Tool incorporation

A calculator, prosthesis, notebook or AI tool can become tightly coupled to action without becoming identical to the biological organism or legal person.

Deletion results:

- boundary typing is necessary;
- control boundary, task-system boundary, organism boundary and identity boundary can diverge;
- capability can extend through external relations without identity incorporation.

Result:

```text
FunctionalIncorporation != OntologicalIdentityMerge
```

### 5.5 Social / legal personhood

Human HF1 already separates organism, subject, self, actor, agent and legal/moral person projections. A legal person can retain institutional status despite biological or psychological facts that are irrelevant to that legal criterion.

This bridges directly into C4 and exposes a deeper distinction:

```text
Causal facts
!=
Constitutive/status facts
```

### 5.6 C3 result

C3 adds two major pressures:

1. **I — representation/content/meaning** cannot be treated as synonymous with evidence or physical state.
2. **Dispositional/modal properties** such as skill/capability are real for prediction/action without being continuously manifested.

Human anti-laws:

1. `Experience != Report`.
2. `Memory != FixedEngramMembership`.
3. `RepresentationPersistence != SubstrateSameness`.
4. `Capability != ManifestPerformance`.
5. `ControlBoundary != OrganismBoundary`.
6. `TaskSystemBoundary != PersonIdentityBoundary`.
7. `CausalDescription != ExhaustiveExperientialDescription` by default.

## 6. C4 — Institutional / economic falsifiers

C4 produces the strongest correction to B.

### 6.1 Corporation

Official company-law practice recognizes incorporated companies as legal entities distinct from their owners/members, capable of entering legal relations. A company can retain legal identity while directors, shareholders, employees, offices, servers and assets change.

Deletion results:

- material bearer: not sufficient;
- process: business/governance processes matter but do not alone establish legal personality;
- relation: rights, membership, control and obligations are constitutive;
- B: accounting, governance and jurisdiction boundaries matter;
- M: law changes permissible actions and powers;
- **new:** legal rules do not merely causally influence the company; they constitute statuses under which an organization counts as a legal person.

This exposes:

```text
K — Constitutive / semantic / deontic status structure
```

### 6.2 Contract

A physical sheet of paper is not by itself the contract. The contract depends on content, parties, assent/formation criteria, governing rules, authority and continuing obligations/powers.

Deletion results:

- I: semantic content cannot be deleted;
- R: party/claim/obligation relations cannot be deleted;
- K: institutional rules constituting obligation cannot be deleted;
- M: obligations/permissions alter action space;
- physical token: often replaceable or absent in oral/electronic agreements.

Key anti-law:

```text
RecordOfContract != ContractRelation
```

### 6.3 Money / bank deposit

Official Bank of England explanations describe modern money largely as currency, bank deposits and central-bank reserves, with bank deposits functioning as IOUs and most money being created through bank lending rather than merely relending pre-existing deposits.

World implication:

- money is not captured by material token identity;
- claim/issuer/holder relations matter;
- acceptance/institutional settlement structure matters;
- balance-sheet representation is evidence/projection of financial reality, not the reality itself;
- K and I are required: the same physical/digital mark has different economic status under different institutional relations.

### 6.4 Market

A market is simultaneously:

- recurring interaction/process;
- network of actors/orders/claims;
- venue/rule institution;
- evolving macro state/projection;
- constrained by capital, liquidity, law and access;
- informationally observed through prices/order data that are not identical to market reality.

Deleting any one of O/R/B/M/E can erase a major explanatory role, but none alone defines `market`.

### 6.5 Jurisdiction / law

Jurisdiction provides a decisive boundary falsifier.

A legal boundary can:

- overlap spatial boundaries;
- apply extraterritorially;
- distinguish persons/entities by status rather than location;
- alter which rules, rights or powers obtain.

Therefore:

```text
Boundary != Geometry
```

while C1 already showed:

```text
PhysicalBoundary sometimes depends essentially on Geometry.
```

World therefore needs typed boundary criteria rather than one generic topology.

### 6.6 Organization under member replacement

Organizations persist despite member turnover when selected legal, governance, role, mission, asset or relational invariants continue. Different criteria can disagree about sameness.

This provides one of the strongest cross-domain confirmations of typed invariance:

```text
SameOrganization_K
= invariance/continuity under K-relevant transformations,
not sameness of members.
```

### 6.7 C4 result — cause is not constitute

This is the major WDF0-C discovery.

B grouped `modal / causal structure` too aggressively. C4 requires at least a three-way separation:

```text
Cause: changing X changes/produces Y through a causal mechanism.
Constraint: X restricts a possibility/trajectory space.
Constitute: under rule/structure R, X counts as or instantiates status Y.
```

Examples:

```text
registration + governing law
constitutes legal-person status;

valid agreement under applicable rules
constitutes contractual obligation;

bank liability under monetary/payment institutions
can constitute deposit money;

authority rule
constitutes permission/power, not merely physical ability.
```

The categories can interact causally, but the explanatory relations are not interchangeable.

Institutional anti-laws:

1. `Cause != Constitute`.
2. `Constraint != ConstitutiveStatus`.
3. `PhysicalAbility != LegalPower`.
4. `InstitutionalStatus != MereBelief`.
5. `InstitutionalStatus != SubstrateIndependentMagic`.
6. `Record != Right/Obligation/Status`.
7. `OrganizationIdentity != MemberIdentity`.
8. `JurisdictionBoundary != SpatialBoundary`.
9. `MoneyToken != MoneyRelation`.
10. `MarketData != MarketReality`.

## 7. C5 — Digital / computational falsifiers

### 7.1 File / content identity

Git provides a clean typed-identity laboratory. Git objects are content-addressed by hashes of object contents; refs are named pointers to objects/commits. Thus different identity questions immediately diverge:

```text
same path?
same bytes/content?
same blob object?
same commit?
same branch/ref?
same repository history?
```

Deleting criterion K from `same file` makes the statement ambiguous.

Result:

```text
IdentifierScheme != UniversalIdentity
```

### 7.2 Process

A running process has temporal realization, state, resources and operating-system identity. The executable file is not the running process; restarting the same binary creates another process occurrence even if higher-level service identity is retained.

Result:

```text
CodeIdentity != ProcessIdentity != ServiceIdentity
```

### 7.3 Distributed service / global state

Distributed systems are a direct attack on naive `one world state at one time` assumptions.

Lamport's work emphasizes causal/partial ordering of events rather than an invariant total event order, and Chandy–Lamport snapshots show how a useful consistent global-state projection can be constructed from distributed local states and communication channels.

Deletion results:

- local process states alone: insufficient for system-level reasoning because in-flight relationships/messages matter;
- one physically privileged global `now`: unnecessary and often unavailable;
- causal ordering: cannot be deleted from many correctness questions;
- E: a snapshot is a constructed, valid projection rather than the system literally stopping in one global instant.

Major anti-laws:

```text
ConsistentGlobalSnapshot != UniquePhysicalGlobalNow
```

```text
TotalTimestampOrder != CausalOrder
```

### 7.4 Git history / rewrite

History is not reducible to current content. Two repositories can have identical working trees but different commit ancestry, signatures, provenance and future merge behavior. Conversely, history can be rewritten while semantic/project continuity may be claimed at another level.

Deletion result:

```text
CurrentState != History
```

in an unusually clean computational case.

### 7.5 Network route

A route is not a static object possession. It is a current relation/path through infrastructure under time-, policy-, endpoint- and owner-dependent conditions.

Current Ordivon World experiments already proved:

```text
HistoricalSuccess != CurrentCapability
ResourceExistence != UsableRelation
UsableRelation != CurrentActionAuthority
```

Network route falsifiers therefore preserve R/B/M/E and typed currentness.

### 7.6 VM migration / checkpointed Agent

A VM or Agent continuity can cross physical substrate, process replacement and storage relocation. This does not prove metaphysical personal identity; it does prove that computational continuity criteria need not equal hardware-material sameness.

World/Security evidence already separates:

- source departure/history;
- continuity carrier;
- destination materialization;
- current Presence;
- subject/cognition binding;
- owner-native observation.

Deletion of these distinctions generates false identity/currentness claims.

### 7.7 C5 result

Digital systems strongly confirm that many apparently ontic nouns are actually **typed identity + model boundary + owner-relative projection**.

They also produce a decisive warning for World Foundations:

```text
State-machine implementation success
is not evidence that Reality itself has one global state machine ontology.
```

Digital anti-laws:

1. `ContentIdentity != PathIdentity != HistoryIdentity`.
2. `Executable != RunningProcess != LogicalService`.
3. `ConsistentSnapshot != UniqueGlobalNow`.
4. `CausalOrder != ArbitraryTotalOrder`.
5. `CurrentTree != RepositoryHistory`.
6. `RouteResource != CurrentUsablePath`.
7. `CheckpointContinuity != HardwareSameness`.
8. `HistoricalMaterialization != CurrentPresence`.


### 8.3 Partial observability

World implication:


### 8.4 Borrowed Body / effector

World W5 and cross-domain experiments show that an Agent can act through a Body/capability whose physical owner remains elsewhere.

Deleting boundary typing causes one of two false collapses:


### 8.5 Multi-owner effect path

### 8.6 Environment changes after model formation

A model that assumes all relevant variables/categories are permanently known can misclassify novelty as noise or impossible data.

```text

not only Bayesian/update-within-a-fixed-schema revision.
```

This is a **meta-epistemic requirement**, not an ontic claim that Reality itself is schema-less.

## 10. The deepest cross-domain convergence — persistence as typed invariance

Across C1–C6, the ordinary noun `same` repeatedly decomposes.

Examples:

- projectile: spatiotemporal/material continuity;
- flame/vortex/wave: organized pattern/process continuity despite constituent turnover;
- organism: organismic/organizational/lineage continuity;
- memory: representational/causal continuity despite engram composition change;
- corporation: legal/organizational status continuity despite member turnover;
- Git blob: exact content identity;
- branch/project: reference/history continuity;
- distributed service: logical service continuity across process/machine replacement;
- VM/Agent: typed continuity across substrate migration.

A candidate World-level reconstruction is therefore:

```text
Persistence_K(x_t1, x_t2, context)
= preservation / continuous transformation of K-relevant invariants,
relations, organization or lineage across an admissible transformation history.
```

This is **not frozen**. It has important unresolved problems:

- who/what defines admissibility?
- must continuity be causal, topological, historical or institutional?
- can identity survive discontinuous reconstruction?
- how does branching/fusion work?
- which invariants are ontic versus query-selected?

But it compresses more cases than `same material`, `same ID` or `same state`.

## 11. Second deep convergence — boundary is a typed relation, often maintained

C shows at least these boundary families:

```text
geometric/material boundary
flux/control boundary
causal/statistical boundary
organizational closure boundary
functional/task boundary
legal/jurisdictional boundary
authority/trust boundary
epistemic/sensor boundary
```

They can overlap and disagree.

A more accurate provisional form is:

```text
Boundary_K(S, Environment, Scale, Time, Criterion)
```

rather than `S has boundary B` as an untyped timeless property.

Further, cells and organizations show:

```text
Boundary can be an effect of maintained organization.
```

This weakens the idea that System must be ontologically prior to process/organization.

## 12. Third deep convergence — causal, modal and constitutive explanation separate

WDF0-B's combined modal/causal family is too coarse.

C requires at least:

### 12.1 Causal

```text
intervention/change in X contributes to change in Y
through mechanism/dependence
```

### 12.2 Modal / constraint

```text
C restricts or structures possible/reachable trajectories/configurations
```

### 12.3 Constitutive

```text
under structure/rule/context R,
configuration X instantiates/counts-as status or whole Y
```

Examples of constitutive claims:

- molecules organized in an ongoing pattern constitute a flame at a selected scale;
- interacting components constitute a cell/organism under organizational criteria;
- a registered organization under law constitutes a legal person;
- relations/records under monetary institutions constitute a bank deposit as money;
- process+resource+protocol organization constitutes a logical service.

Constitution can itself have causal effects, but:

```text
Cause != Constitute
Constitute != Constraint
```

This distinction is admitted into the WDF0 grammar as a **required explanatory firewall**.

## 13. Fourth convergence — history can be constitutive

C repeatedly falsifies `history = metadata`.

History matters for:

- hysteresis and alternative stable states;
- biological development;
- evolutionary lineage/species;
- memory/learning;
- legal provenance and contracts;
- Git ancestry;
- distributed causality;
- Agent evidence/currentness and recovery.

Therefore:

```text
SameCurrentProjection
!=
SameRealityForEveryQuestion
```

when the histories differ.

World must retain a distinction between:

```text
current configuration
trajectory/history
provenance/genealogy
```

without assuming that every query needs full history.

## 14. Fifth convergence — scale is not presentation-only

Scale determines which structures even become available to a model:

- vortex / wave pattern;
- thermodynamic variables;
- phase/order parameter;
- organism organization;
- ecosystem regime;
- corporation/market;
- distributed logical service.

Some macro projections can improve prediction/control or correspond to institutional/organizational invariants that are absent from a raw micro description.

Causal-emergence work also explicitly studies cases in which coarse-grained macro dynamics exhibit stronger measured effective causal structure than micro dynamics, while acknowledging that results can depend on coarse-graining choice.

WDF0 therefore retains:

```text
ScaleChoice can change valid explanatory variables.
```

But not:

```text
Every macro description is ontologically fundamental.
```

## 15. Sixth convergence — physical realization and abstract structure must not collapse

C1 and C5 together expose a useful firewall.

Two systems may be structurally isomorphic at one abstraction while differing in:

- physical quantities;
- locality/geometry;
- energy/resource costs;
- latency;
- failure modes;
- legal/authority substrate;
- embodied affordances.

Therefore:

```text
StructuralIsomorphism != Physical/InstitutionalEquivalence
```

World Foundations need an explicit route from abstract relation/dynamics to **realization substrate and constraints** when those matter.

## 16. Seventh convergence — information has at least two truth roles

C exposes why `Information` cannot be one primitive.

### Role A — physical/statistical structure

Correlations, signals, encoded patterns and channel states can be physically instantiated.

### Role B — representational/semantic content

A memory, contract, message, financial claim or model can be about something or count as something under interpretive/institutional relations.

### Role C — epistemic evidence

A signal/record can support or fail to support a belief/claim.

Thus:

```text
PhysicalPattern
!= SemanticContent
!= EvidenceRole
```

The same token can occupy all three roles in different relations.

WDF0-C therefore does not solve information ontology; it proves that `information` must be decomposed later.

## 17. Eighth convergence — Agent world models require structural revisability

A mature World model cannot merely maintain probability distributions over a fixed ontology.

Real Agent failure can occur because:

```text
wrong parameter
wrong current state
wrong transition law
wrong boundary
wrong entity type
missing relation
missing action
missing sensor
missing institutional rule
wrong scale
wrong ontology
```

Therefore World-model learning has at least two levels:

```text
WithinModelUpdate:
  update state/belief/parameters inside current schema

ModelRevision:
  add/remove/split/merge concepts, relations, boundaries,
  variables, mechanisms or scales
```

This is likely central to Ordivon's long-term `world model changes through practice` principle.

## 18. What WDF0-C actually falsifies

C now rejects stronger claims that B could only suspect.

### F1 — Material-bearer persistence is universal

Rejected by flame, vortex, organism, memory, corporation and distributed service.

### F2 — Current configuration contains all explanatory information

Rejected by hysteresis, development, lineage, memory, Git history and distributed causal order.

### F3 — Boundary is an intrinsic static shell

### F4 — State-space coordinates are fixed by Reality rather than modeling scope

### F5 — Cause and constraint are enough for institutional reality

### F7 — Information/evidence/meaning are one kind

Rejected by memory, contract, money, messages and world-model evidence.

Rejected as a default by phase, organism, ecosystem, corporation, market and logical-service cases where macro variables/organization determine valid explanation/action at that scale.

### F9 — A complete Agent world model only needs fixed-schema belief updates

Rejected by environment/model structural change.

## 24. WDF0-C closeout

WDF0-C closes with these durable research conclusions:

```text
1. No single ontic noun survived as universal root.
2. Several orthogonal explanatory roles survive deletion across radically different realities.
3. Persistence is increasingly well modeled as typed invariance/continuity under transformation rather than material or ID equality.
4. Boundary is typed, scale-sensitive and sometimes maintained by organization rather than given in advance.
5. History/path dependence can be constitutive, not metadata.
6. Cause, constraint and constitution must be separated.
7. Physical realization requires locality/geometry and typed quantities/flows when relevant.

```

No production refactor is justified by these conclusions. WDF0-D is admitted; WDF1 remains UNKNOWN.
