# 1. Objective

WDF2-L established that counterfactual supposition dynamically transforms a typed context and that one still-unfounded component repeatedly influences premise retention, modal restriction, alternative selection, explanation and later actual-causation judgments:

```text
normality
usualness
typicality
default
expectation
plausibility
norm
baseline
```

WDF2-M asks:

> **Are these one structure, several related structures, or merely psychologically conflated labels—and which of them, if any, are entitled to constrain counterfactual generation, ordering, context update, inference or downstream causal attribution?**

The round deliberately refuses to define `normal` as:

```text
high probability
most frequent
average
expected
mechanistically default
legally permitted
morally ideal
status quo
closest
salient
relevant
```

without falsification.

# 3. Human use of “normal” is already hybrid

Bear and Knobe's experimental work shows that ordinary judgments of what is `normal` are influenced by both descriptive averages and prescriptive ideals.

This is evidence about human concept representation.

It does **not** prove that descriptive and prescriptive normality are one property in Reality.

### Earned firewall M-PSY-1

```text
PsychologicalFusionOfNormalityDimensions
!= OntologicalIdentityOfThoseDimensions
```

---

# 4. Norm effects can be cognitively contaminated

Güver and Kneer's 2025 preregistered experiments show that norm violations can increase perceived causation even when the norms are nonpertinent or deliberately silly; they argue this favors a blame-driven Bias View over several stronger semantic/responsibility interpretations.

### Strong firewall M-BIAS-1

```text
NormInfluencesHumanCounterfactualOrCausalJudgment
!= NormIsSemanticallyJustifiedSelectionCriterion
```

Behavioral sensitivity is evidence about cognition, not automatic evidence about counterfactual truth.

# PART I — FREQUENCY

# 5. Frequency

For event/property E in a reference population/class R and interval T:

```text
Freq(E | R,T)
```

measures observed proportion/count.

---

# 6. Frequency is reference-class dependent

A behavior can be frequent among:

```text
all users
power users
users in one jurisdiction
one software version
one historical period
```

and rare in another class.

### Earned firewall M-FREQ-1

```text
Frequency != ContextFreePropertyOfEventType
```

---

# 7. Frequency is not permission

A prohibited workaround can be used by 90% of operators.

```text
Frequent
AND
Forbidden
```

is coherent.

### Earned firewall M-FREQ-2

```text
Frequent != Permitted
```

---

# 8. Frequency is not mechanistic default

A failover mechanism can be the system's designated default under failure while failures themselves are extremely rare.

```text
RareEvent
can trigger
DefaultMechanism
```

---

# PART II — STATISTICAL TYPICALITY

# 9. Typicality need not equal raw frequency

A value can be `typical` in the sense of central/prototypical without being the most frequent exact value.

For continuous variables, every exact value can have negligible probability while regions around a central value are typical.

### Earned firewall M-TYP-1

```text
Typicality != ExactValueFrequency
```

---

# 10. Average is not typical by definition

A bimodal distribution can have a mean lying in a low-density region.

Thus:

```text
Mean != Mode != Median != TypicalInstance
```

No statistical baseline is universal.

---

# 11. Typicality depends on representation/grain

At one grain:

```text
“ordinary web request”
```

may be typical.

At a finer grain, the exact header/timing/route tuple may be unique.

### Earned firewall M-GRAIN-1

```text
TypicalAtGrain_g != TypicalAtAllFinerGrains
```

---

# PART III — PROBABILITY / OBJECTIVE CHANCE

# 12. High probability is one possible typicality signal

If an objective stochastic mechanism supplies a measure:

```text
P(E)
```

high-probability regions may reasonably count as statistically typical.

But WDF1 remains binding:

```text
ProbabilityMeasure != ProbabilityInterpretation
```

---

# 13. High probability is not normality simpliciter

A legally forbidden behavior can be highly probable.

A morally required rescue may be statistically unlikely.

A deterministic model can assign probability one to actual evolution while counterfactual practice still distinguishes ordinary from abnormal interventions.

### Earned firewall M-PROB-1

```text
HighProbability != NormalByEveryRelevantSense
```

---

# 14. Probability zero is not maximal abnormality

Continuous events can have probability zero at exact points without being impossible or especially abnormal.

WDF1 firewall survives:

```text
ProbabilityZero != Impossibility
```

and WDF2-M adds:

```text
ProbabilityZero != AutomaticMaximalAbnormality
```

# PART IV — PREDICTIVE EXPECTATION

# 15. Prediction

An agent/model can expect E because its predictive model assigns E high posterior probability.

This is epistemically indexed.

---

# 16. Expected does not equal objective typical

A poorly calibrated agent can expect an objectively rare event.

Conversely, an ignorant agent can fail to expect a highly typical event.

### Earned firewall M-EXP-1

```text
ExpectedByAgent != ObjectivelyTypical
```

---

# 17. Surprise is epistemic

`Surprising to observer O` can be valuable for explanation/cognition but does not become a fundamental modal property of the event.

### Earned firewall M-SURP-1

```text
Surprising != AbnormalInEverySense
```

---

# PART V — EPISTEMIC PLAUSIBILITY / RANKING

# 18. Ranking-theoretic plausibility

Spohn-style ranking functions represent degrees of disbelief/plausibility and support sophisticated conditional reasoning.

This is an epistemic structure.

---

# 19. Rank is not frequency

Two propositions can have the same ordinal plausibility rank while radically different numerical probabilities.

Ranking theory is intentionally not reducible to ordinary probability representation.

### Earned firewall M-RANK-1

```text
PlausibilityRank != Frequency != ProbabilityValue
```

---

# 20. Epistemic plausibility is not moral normality

A morally forbidden act can be highly plausible.

A heroic act can be morally ideal and epistemically implausible.

---

# 21. Ranking is a carrier, not a grounding source

An ordering can encode normality/plausibility judgments once supplied.

It does not answer why one world should outrank another.

### Strong firewall M-CARRIER-1

```text
OrderingRepresentation != OrderingGrounding
```

This applies equally to Lewisian similarity orderings, Halpern/Hitchcock normality orderings and Spohn ranks.

# PART VI — MECHANISTIC DEFAULT

# 22. Mechanistic default

A system can have a designated or naturally arising transition when no special disturbance/override occurs.

Examples:

```text
spring returns to equilibrium
network route follows default path
controller uses nominal policy
program executes default branch
```

---

# 23. Mechanistic default is not frequency

A backup controller can be explicitly defined as default under emergency mode despite emergency mode being rare.

Likewise, frequent manual overrides do not rewrite the mechanism's nominal default.

### Earned firewall M-MECH-1

```text
MechanisticDefault != MostFrequentObservedOutcome
```

---

# 24. Mechanistic default is model/regime relative

Change the mechanism or regime and the default can change.

```text
DefaultUnderMechanismM
!= DefaultUnderMechanismM'
```

---

# 25. No-disturbance baseline is substantive

Calling one transition the `default` often assumes a privileged notion of:

```text
no intervention
no disturbance
no override
```

which itself requires a baseline specification.

This becomes one route into WDF2-N.

# PART VII — CAUSAL-MODEL DEFAULT

# 26. Structural-equation default

A modeler may mark one value of variable X as `normal/default`.

Halpern/Hitchcock explicitly distinguish defaults/typical variable values and then lift them into a normality ordering over worlds.

---

# 27. Model default is not Reality default

The selected variable values, exogenous/endogenous distinction and normality assumptions are modeling choices with provenance.

### Earned firewall M-MODEL-1

```text
ModelDefault != RealityDefault by declaration
```

---

# 28. Useful modeling choices can still be objective-constrained

A model default can be warranted by:

```text
mechanism
statistics
institutional rule
engineering specification
```

but the warrant must be exposed rather than hidden inside `normal`.

# 29. Configuration default

Software can literally specify:

```text

FEATURE_X = false unless configured
```

This is an implementation/specification fact.

# 30. Config default can be statistically atypical

### Earned firewall M-SW-1


# 31. Default can change by version

The same key can have different default values across software releases.

# 33. Convention is not moral norm

A conventional default can be morally neutral.

# 34. Convention is institution/community relative

# PART X — INSTITUTIONAL / LEGAL NORM

# 35. Institutional norm

A rule system can prescribe, permit, forbid or require acts/statuses.

This is constitutive/normative structure, not a statistical summary.

---

# 36. Legal norm vs actual compliance

```text
LegallyRequired(A)
```


### Earned firewall M-LEGAL-1

# 37. Legal normality can affect institutional counterfactuals

For queries about:


But this is query-role grounded.

---

# 38. Norm is not cause

A legal rule can alter institutional status constitutively and influence agent behavior causally through enforcement, but the **normative property as such** is not automatically a physical cause.

### Earned firewall M-NORMCAUSE-1

```text
NormativeStatus != PhysicalCause
```

WDF0 cause/constitution separation remains binding.

# PART XI — MORAL / PRESCRIPTIVE NORM

# 39. Moral norm

A moral norm concerns what ought/ought not be done under an ethical standard.

---

# 40. Moral ideal can be statistically rare

```text
MorallyIdeal
AND
StatisticallyRare
```

is coherent.

---

# 41. Moral norm can influence human causal judgment

The Knobe-effect literature and Halpern/Hitchcock motivation show this robustly.

But WDF2-M separates:

```text
psychological effect
semantic relevance
normative justification
```

# 42. 2025 bias pressure is decisive

If irrelevant/silly norm violations also raise causal ratings, then at least some observed norm sensitivity cannot safely be treated as evidence that the norm tracks the objective causal/counterfactual structure.

### Strong firewall M-BIAS-2


# PART XII — DESCRIPTIVE HABIT

# 43. Habit

An individual/organization may usually perform action A.

---

# 44. Habit is not population frequency

Personal habitual norm and population norm differ.

```text
TypicalForActor
!= TypicalForPopulation
```

---

# 45. Habit is not obligation

People routinely violate their own habits without violating a moral/legal norm.

# PART XIII — PROTOTYPE / CATEGORY NORMALITY

# 46. Prototypicality

An instance can be a prototypical bird, tool or failure case despite not being the modal statistical instance.

This belongs to conceptual/category representation.

---

# 47. Prototype is not causal default

A prototypical server failure example need not be produced by the most common failure mechanism.

### Earned firewall M-PROT-1

A chosen baseline can be intentionally abnormal.

### Earned firewall M-BASE-1

```text
ReferenceBaseline != NormalState by definition
```

This dependence becomes the strongest residual.

# PART XIX — CONTROLLABILITY / MUTABILITY

# 60. Human mutability effects

Byrne/McCloy and related work shows people preferentially mentally undo controllable and inappropriate actions in many counterfactual tasks.

---

# 61. Mutability is function-sensitive

This may be useful for:

```text
learning
regret
future corrective action
policy search
```

rather than expressing a universal metaphysical ordering over worlds.

---

# 62. Fazelpour functional account

Fazelpour argues that counterfactual selectivity should be understood functionally and develops a qualitative decision-theoretic account tied to identifying corrective policies after bad outcomes.

This gives a principled route for some norm sensitivity:

```text
norms can matter because they help select actionable/corrective alternatives for a task
```

rather than because norms are automatically part of counterfactual truth.

### Earned firewall M-FUNC-1

```text
FunctionalUsefulnessForCounterfactualGeneration
!= UniversalCounterfactualTruthCondition
```

# PART XX — HALPERN/HITCHCOCK NORMALITY ORDERINGS

# 63. Extended causal model

Halpern/Hitchcock enrich structural causal models with a normality relation over worlds.

Their framework is deliberately flexible enough to capture defaults, typicality and normality.

---

# 64. Partial preorder

A central design choice is that the normality relation may be a **partial preorder**.

Thus two worlds can be incomparable in normality.

### Strong result M-PARTIAL-1

```text
NormalityOrderingNeedNotBeTotal
```

This is foundation-important because forcing a scalar score can fabricate comparisons unsupported by the domain.

---

# 65. Partial order is a representation, not one normality theory

A partial preorder can encode:

```text
statistical defaults
moral norms
institutional expectations
mechanistic defaults
hybrids
```

but does not specify how those sources combine.

---

# 66. Graded causation is downstream

Halpern/Hitchcock use normality to rank witness worlds and obtain graded/comparative actual-causation judgments.

WDF2-M does not import this actual-causation consequence yet.

It extracts only the representation lesson.

---

# 67. Normality can stabilize modeling but not eliminate modeling judgment

Halpern's later work shows normality assumptions can improve stability under causal-model refinement.

Still, choice of variables/model/default assumptions remains provenance-bearing.

---

# PART XXI — ICARD / KOMINSKY / KNOBE PROBABILISTIC SAMPLING

# 68. Sampling account

Icard, Kominsky and Knobe explain several normality effects on causal judgment using probabilistic sampling in graphical causal models.

This is evidence that human causal judgment can emerge from a probabilistic computational process rather than a primitive moral-normality operator.

---

# 69. Psychological process vs semantic foundation

### Earned firewall M-COG-1

```text
SuccessfulCognitiveModelOfNormalityEffect
!= ProofOfCounterfactualMetaphysics
```

A sampling account predicts judgments; it does not by itself establish what makes a counterfactual true.

# PART XXV — LOCAL RENORMALIZATION AFTER SUPPOSITION

# 82. Abnormal antecedent problem

Suppose A is globally very abnormal.

Once A is explicitly stipulated, how should later alternatives be ranked?

---

# 83. Policy 1 — retain global penalty

Continue comparing A-worlds partly by how abnormal A is relative to actuality.

Useful when query asks:

```text
how extraordinary would the whole scenario be?
which witness is less abnormal globally?
```

---

# 84. Policy 2 — antecedent-conditioned renormalization

Treat A as fixed/given and compare **within the A-domain**:

```text
Normality(w | A)
```

Useful when query asks:

```text
given A, what would normally happen next?
```

---

# 85. These are different questions

### Strong firewall M-RENORM-1

```text
GlobalAntecedentAbnormality
!= WithinAntecedentNormalityOrdering
```

and:

```text
Suppose(A)
does not determine
whether A's own abnormality penalty is retained in downstream ranking.
```

Compressed result:

> **WDF2-M finds that `normality` is not one modal primitive or one defensible scalar ordering. Statistical frequency, distributional typicality, predictive expectation, objective chance, epistemic plausibility, mechanistic default, model/configuration default, convention, institutional/legal norm, moral norm, personal habit, prototype, status quo and comparison baseline can all diverge. Human cognition often fuses descriptive and prescriptive information under the word `normal`, but that psychological fusion is not ontological identity; the 2025 finding that even nonpertinent and silly norm violations increase causal ratings provides a direct warning that observed norm effects can reflect blame/salience bias rather than semantically justified counterfactual selection. Normality-like information can legitimately affect counterfactual reasoning in different roles—generation priority, soft ordering, rule-preserving admissibility, premise retention, local renormalization, reporting, corrective-policy search and downstream causal-witness ranking—but these roles must remain distinct. Halpern/Hitchcock demonstrate that partial preorders over worlds are a viable representation and that incomparability is often preferable to fabricated scalar tradeoffs, yet an ordering is only a carrier: it does not ground whether frequency, mechanism, law, convention or morality should determine the comparison. WDF2-M therefore reconstructs normality as a typed, provenance-bearing multi-dimensional profile with optional partial comparison and explicit query-role routing. It also separates global antecedent abnormality from conditional normality within an already-supposed antecedent: an antecedent may remain globally extraordinary while downstream outcomes are ranked normally within the antecedent-local domain. The deepest remaining gap is now unavoidable: every form of normality depends on a reference scope, and counterfactual effect/contrast itself depends on a baseline or foil. Which population, regime, role, version, comparator or alternative set is appropriate cannot be answered by the word `normal`. WDF2 therefore advances next to baseline, reference class, contrast and comparison-set foundations.**
