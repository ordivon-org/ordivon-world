# 1. The abundant-classification problem

For almost any target universe U, indefinitely many predicates/classes can be constructed.

Examples:

```text
K1 = objects with mass in interval M
K2 = objects currently in room R
K3 = electrons in lab L OR red artifacts built on Tuesday
K4 = objects whose identifiers hash to an even integer
K5 = members of an admitted biological lineage
K6 = tokens recognized as valid currency under institution I
```

Each can have:

```text
explicit criterion
factual extension
perfectly decidable membership in favorable cases
```

Yet they do not appear to have the same ontological/scientific/institutional significance.

This is structurally the same pressure Lewis identifies against an abundant-property conception: arbitrary classifications are too cheap to perform the work required of objective similarity, causal relevance, lawhood or naturalness.

### I starting law


# 2. Gerrymandering is not a syntactic property

A tempting rule is:

```text
simple/non-disjunctive predicate = natural
complex/disjunctive predicate    = gerrymandered
```

This fails.

A syntactically complex criterion may track one deep mechanism.
A syntactically simple criterion may be arbitrary.

More importantly, a previously arbitrary-looking class can become Reality-significant if a new mechanism/rule/protocol begins to couple its members.

Example pattern:

```text
At t1:
  K* = A OR B
  no shared support relation

At t2:
  control system/institution/protocol P
  treats exactly all K* members under one operative rule
  and creates common consequences
```

Then K* acquires at least a new functional/institutional significance at t2.

### Anti-law I1

```text
DisjunctiveSyntax != OntologicalArbitrariness
```

### Anti-law I2


Institutional facts make this especially clear.

# 5. Why projectibility alone fails

Projectibility asks whether learning that some members have property P licenses induction about other members.

This is important scientific evidence.

But it is not universal.

Problems:

```text
1. A class can be projectible only inside a narrow environment/CVE.
2. Engineered datasets can make arbitrary labels predictive.
3. Institutional kinds can be objectively significant even when they do not support broad natural-science induction.
4. Some real classifications may be historically unique and poorly projectible.
```

### Verdict

```text
Projectibility = strong SEI in some domains
NOT universal RSB
```

# 23. Shared Support Requirement

I introduces:

# **SSR-alpha — Shared Support Requirement**

A classification H is not admitted as Reality-significant merely because every member separately has some interesting property.

H must identify at least one support architecture that:

```text
1. applies across the classified members under the stated criterion/CVE;
2. is independently evidenced or constituted;
3. discriminates the classification from relevant alternatives/nonmembers;
4. generates, constrains, preserves, tracks or constitutes consequences
   not obtained merely from repeating the membership stipulation.
```

This blocks arbitrary disjunctions whose only unity is the disjunction itself.

# 83. Final compressed judgment

WDF3-I rejects the idea that objective carving can be captured by one universal notion of naturalness. Projectibility, stable clustering, causal mechanisms, laws, symmetry, intervention, robustness, compression/real-pattern success, genealogy, function, specification and institutional constitution each identify real modes or indicators of classification significance, but none is both necessary and sufficient across physics, biology, artifacts, institutions and Agent-era systems.

The key reconstruction is a strict Support/Evidence separation. Predictive success, compression, projectibility and robustness can be evidence that a classification tracks Reality, but they are not by themselves what makes the classification significant. The constitutive/support basis can instead be physical, causal, structural, genealogical, functional, specification-based or institutional. This permits human-created categories to be objectively real when operative rules or systems create actual consequences, while preventing mere linguistic stipulation from manufacturing ontology.

Gerrymanderedness is therefore not a syntactic property of predicates or an immutable property of extensions. An arbitrary disjunction remains unsupported if its only unity is the disjunction itself; the same extension can later acquire task-local or institutional significance if a real control mechanism, protocol or constitutive authority begins treating it as one consequential class. What matters is shared, differential, noncircular-or-operatively-constitutive support that yields nontrivial consequences within a stated CVE.

This yields the provisional Constraint-Coupled Significance principle and Reality Significance Profile. They allow multiple overlapping objective carvings with different support modes without collapsing into relativism or a scalar naturalness score. Equivalence/quotient naturalness becomes a special case: differences may be quotiented only when a typed support profile shows they are irrelevant to the target claim while preserving target-criterion adjudicability.

I therefore resolves H's immediate conventionalism threat without restoring primitive Kind. But it does not yet close WDF3. The explanatory burden has moved one level deeper: `support` itself can be used circularly or gerrymandered. WDF3-J must determine what makes a causal, structural, historical, functional, specification or constitutive support relation legitimate, and whether that analysis forces the return of generic Grounding/Building or instead preserves a typed family. Until that succeeds, WDF3 remains open.
