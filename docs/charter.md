# Ordivon Edge Charter

Status: active product interpretation of Computing `EDGE-CHARTER-003`

## Mission

Ordivon Edge is the Task-to-external-execution continuity overlay above mature
external providers. It translates an open Task's current execution need into an
exact provider binding, preserves the identity and uncertainty of remote work,
exports durable results, and permits the Task to continue after body or provider
replacement.

## Candidate responsibility

Edge researches and may own:

- Placement Requirement and Provider Capability Observation;
- immutable Placement Binding to exact provider execution;
- accepted/running/success/failure/unknown remote outcome reconciliation;
- Artifact and Observation provenance across temporary bodies;
- minimum sufficient semantic reconstruction;
- multi-body provenance and residual closure.

Exact Task, Attempt, Effect, and Dispatch semantics remain foreign references
owned by Host or the semantic Kernel. Provider-native body and Sandbox lifecycle
remain owned by the provider.

## Current evidence

The Cloudflare provider is real production infrastructure. Its transaction,
Receipt, Artifact, policy, release, and reconciliation mechanisms are strong
evidence for remote-effect reliability.

The provider-neutral Node lifecycle and local `unshare` body are a bounded
research/conformance substrate. They do not by themselves establish a durable
Agent-presence layer.

## Boundary

Edge supplies where and through which external provider an Effect executes and
how that execution remains attributable and recoverable. Host decides why the
Task acts and when to change strategy. Runtime owns trusted-local execution.
Link owns connectivity. Security or the domain system owns consequence policy
and final validity.

## Success condition

One open Task can use, replace, or parallelize external bodies; preserve exact
Effect/Dispatch/provider execution history; reconcile ambiguous results; export
sufficient Artifacts and evidence; classify residue; and continue without
reimplementing the underlying provider.
